# [diffusion] perf: move MiniMax-H3's pipelined Ulysses copies off the SMs

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/pull/43361) · state: closed · merged: True

## Original PR description

Follow-up to #43164 (merged). The branch carries #43164's original commits and a merge of main, so against main the diff is only this change.

## Motivation

After #43164 (and #43327 / #43337), a profile of MiniMax-H3's pipelined Ulysses attention shows almost no exposed exchange left: 0.09–0.24 ms per layer. What remains on the SMs is the exchange's own data movement. Per head group and destination, two PyTorch strided copies run:

- v into the exchange block, from the fill;
- this rank's and each peer's rows into the merged output.

That is 28 elementwise copy kernels per layer at Ulysses 2 and 56 at Ulysses 4. Measured alone they move 0.8–1.4 TB/s (read plus write), and inside the pipeline they compete with FlashAttention, which wants every SM. Their SM time per layer, from isolated timings at the real shapes:

| Case (rows per rank) | merge copy | v copy | per layer | share of the layer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Ref2VA, Ulysses 2 (24826) | 35.7 µs | 37.6 µs | 14 × 73 µs = 1.03 ms | 1.2% |
| T2VA, Ulysses 2 (18871) | 28.6 µs | 30.2 µs | 14 × 59 µs = 0.82 ms | 1.5% |
| T2VA, Ulysses 4 (9436) | 12.2 µs | 12.6 µs | 28 × 25 µs = 0.69 ms | 2.4% |

## Modifications

Every copy of the exchange now runs on the copy engine, as a pitched `cuMemcpy2DAsync` issued next to the stream memory operations it already uses. Nothing moves on the SMs, and the result stays bit-identical: it is pure data movement, and attention sees the same values.

- **Receive slots are split by part:** `[2, groups, 3, world, chunk]`. Group g's q, k and v from every rank are each one contiguous `[S, group_heads, head_dim]` tensor, and attention reads them directly.
- **v moves straight from the projection output** into every destination's receive slot, this rank's own included. It no longer passes through the send buffer.
- **q/k:**
  - With a `fill` (H3's QK-norm), the fill writes q and k into the send buffer, and one 2-row copy takes both to the peer.
  - Without a `fill`, q, k and v all move straight from their sources, and the pack kernel is gone.
- **The merge:** this rank's rows and each peer's rows land in their output block with a pitched copy on the group stream.
- **`fill(head_start, head_count, q_dst, k_dst)`** now writes only q and k.
- **Memory:** the send buffer holds q/k only, so the buffers are 10 × (rows per rank) × heads × head_dim instead of 11 ×, which is 3.6 GB for Ref2VA at Ulysses 2. The cookbook note says so.

## Accuracy Tests

- `test_ipc_a2a_multi_gpu.py` passes at 2 and 4 ranks. It compares against the sequential exchange byte for byte, with and without a fill, including the automatic group count and the head count that must decline.
- End to end on 4×H200, the MP4 md5 is unchanged in all twelve runs: T2VA at Ulysses 2 and 4, and Ref2VA at Ulysses 2.

## Speed Tests and Profiling

Client end-to-end latency, 4×H200, 50 steps, default quality. These runs sit on top of #43164, #43327 and #43337. The arms ran ABBA on one box (without, with, with, without):

| Case | without | with | e2e | denoise stage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| T2VA 5 s, 4 GPUs, Ulysses 4 | 71.66 / 71.69 s | 70.88 / 70.95 s | −1.06% | 69.88 → 69.09 s (−1.13%) |
| T2VA 5 s, 2 GPUs, Ulysses 2 | 140.40 / 140.38 s | 139.91 / 139.87 s | −0.36% | 136.63 → 135.82 s (−0.59%) |
| Ref2VA 5 s, 2 GPUs, Ulysses 2 | 218.50 / 218.74 s | 217.82 / 218.00 s | −0.32% | 212.27 → 211.07 s (−0.57%) |

The gain is largest at Ulysses 4, which ran 56 copy kernels per layer against 28 at Ulysses 2. It is below the SM time the copies held, because part of that time overlapped attention tails anyway, and the H200s sit at their 700 W cap, so part of the freed time comes back as a lower clock.
