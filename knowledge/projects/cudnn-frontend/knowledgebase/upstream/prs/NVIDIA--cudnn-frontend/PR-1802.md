# frost(gated_attention_block): the block-sparse decode split rule removes the wave tail -- where the fill rule leaves a second wave the split minimizes waves x (tiles per chunk + 1); every one-wave schedule keeps its measured split

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cudnn-frontend/pull/1802) · state: closed · merged: True

## Original PR description

On `develop` (33bee405f: #1789's in-block indexer decode step and #1792's DSL 4.7.0 fixes merged): ONE commit over `develop`, the
block-sparse DECODE mode's default split rule.  No binding / tracker / Capabilities change (a knob-domain default only, Rule S2 exempt:
`decode_split_kv` stays the one served knob, any split in `[1, ceil((top_k + 1) / 32)]` pins the former choice).

## Summary

The block-sparse decode mode (`kv_source="paged"` on a `QsaSpec` geometry) chooses its split-KV when the declaration leaves
`decode_split_kv=None`.  The former FILL rule -- `ceil(SMs / (B x H_kv x S_q))` chunks per work item, capped at the item's tile count and
at 16 -- puts one chunk-item per SM but does not see the tail wave it leaves where the chunk-items overflow one wave of SMs: a CTA streams
its chunk serially, so 256 equal items on 212 SMs cost two item durations, not 1.2.  The block's end-to-end census on a Rubin cc 10.7 part
(212 SMs at the 2376 MHz lock, CUDA-graph replay, the 24 / 2 geometry at `top_k` 512) showed three schedules left in two waves: the
T 128 step's (B 32 x S_q 4) 256 unsplit 17-tile items, B 32 x S_q 1 at split 4 (256 chunk-items in 1.2 waves) and B 4 x S_q 4 at split 7
(224 chunk-items in 1.06 waves).

`sparse_decode_split_choice` gains the WAVE rule: where the fill rule's pick overflows one wave (`units x split > SMs`) the split in
`[1, min(ceil((top_k + 1) / 32), SPARSE_DECODE_SPLIT_CAP)]` that minimizes `ceil(units x split / SMs) x (ceil(tiles / split) +
SPARSE_DECODE_SPLIT_ITEM_COST_TILES)` -- the waves the launch takes, times the tiles a chunk streams plus the chunk-item's fixed cost
(`sparse_decode_split_rounds`; the cost constant is ONE tile, a model term: the bare `waves x tiles` count would cut the 17-tile T 128 item
into nine two-tile chunks) -- ties to the smaller split.  On a 212-SM part: T 128 1 -> 3 (768 chunk-items of 6 / 6 / 5 tiles), B 32 x
S_q 1 4 -> 3 (192 chunk-items, one wave), B 4 x S_q 4 7 -> 6 (192, one wave).  Every launch the fill rule keeps in one wave -- every
B <= 4 x S_q 1 cell and B 1 x S_q 4, the measured 16s -- is untouched, bitwise.  The combine form a moved cell takes is the pinned one
(the lean group walk at B 32 x 24 heads at split 3 as at 4; the combine module's own forms at B 4 x S_q 4 at split 6 as at 7).  A
performance choice only: the same function under every value.
