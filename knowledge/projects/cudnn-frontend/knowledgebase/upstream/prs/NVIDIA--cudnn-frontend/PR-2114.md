## Summary

On cc 10.3 the default dispatch ranks `sdpa_bwd_sm100_d256` behind the backend for every graph. `bwd/heuristics._d256_leads` was measured on cc 10.0 only, and `PART_TRANSFERS` names no backward study. A frozen study on a B300 (Rule S12) found the FROST row faster on most d256 bwd graphs there. This PR adds the entries that qualified on their holdouts: bf16 THD graphs, on the 128-key tile and off it.

`placement_table.TABLE` gains sixteen cc 10.3 entries for `sdpa_bwd_sm100_d256`, bf16, (256, 256), prefill, THD and ragged THD, top-left causal and mask-free, GQA and MHA. S_kv on and off the 128-key tile are separate classes (`class_key`), and each on-tile class has its off-tile twin, as `test_kv_off_the_tile_is_its_own_class_with_its_own_entry` requires.

- On the tile (study `sm103_d256_bwd_v2_20261031`):
  - always: THD causal GQA, ragged causal GQA, ragged mask-free GQA;
  - cost proxy >= 28.0: THD causal MHA;
  - cost proxy >= 28.5: THD mask-free GQA and MHA, ragged causal MHA, ragged mask-free MHA.
- Off the tile (study `sm103_d256_bwd_thd_offtile_20261031`):
  - always: six classes;
  - cost proxy >= 30.374: THD mask-free MHA and ragged mask-free MHA.

`benchmark/placement_gen/gen.py --emit` emitted each block from its study's records. Dense cc 10.3 graphs get no entry and keep trailing the backend. So do f16, deterministic, bias, dropout, window and sink graphs. cc 10.0 is unchanged: no cc 10.0 entry is added, so `_d256_leads` still decides there.

Tests:
- `test_cc103_d256_backward_thd_entries_are_its_own` covers, on and off the tile:
  - a cc 10.3 lookup;
  - the same graph on cc 10.0 (no entry);
  - both sides of each cost threshold;
  - dense and f16 graphs (no entry).
- `test_sm100_d256_leads_the_backend_only_inside_its_measured_box` adds the cc 10.3 THD ordering on and off the tile, and the features no study measured.

## Why

On the qualified on-tile classes the default dispatch has a holdout regret of 1.273 geomean, and 22 of 24 cases run more than 5% slower than the better leg. Off the tile it is 1.298, with 23 of 24 cases more than 5% slower. With the entries, 23 of the 24 on-tile THD holdout cases run the FROST row first. On 22 of them it takes 0.62-0.90 of the backend's time; the 23rd (S 896, b 3, MHA 6/6, mask-free) is at parity (1.025). The one case left to the backend (S 384, b 1, MHA 6/6) would have taken 1.021 of its time on FROST. Off the tile, the entries pick the faster leg on all 24 holdout cases (regret 1.000).

## The studies

All three studies share one setup. B300 SXM6 (1100 W, one exclusive GPU), develop `64ed3dfe`, backend leg on cuDNN 9.28.0.38 (`nvidia-cudnn-cu13==9.28.0.38.dev75998618`), bf16, d 256/256.

Each case runs the three legs of `benchmark/attention_training/benchmark_single_sdpa.py`, `--profile_pass bwd`: `cudnn` (backend only), `cudnn_oss` (FROST pinned) and `cudnn_auto` (default dispatch). Each leg times 20 iterations after 5 warmup, and the leg order rotates per case. Each sweep started on a GPU idle for 120 s and held it for its whole run.

### On the tile: `sm103_d256_bwd_v2_20261031`

- Train, 192 cases:
  - The v1 grid: GQA 16/2, 32/4 and MHA 16/16; S 1k-16k at 32k tokens; top-left causal and mask-free; BSHD, THD at equal lengths, and THD half-to-full. 90 cases, sha256 `c2c4271c…`.
  - v1's holdout: 30 off-lattice cases, sha256 `017a112a…`. It was used once to qualify v1 and is folded into training here.
  - The extremes each class's entry answers for: S 128 / 256 / 512 / 2048 / 32768 at b 1-8, and MQA 32/1 and G32 64/2 at S 4096. 72 cases, sha256 `17ce7bbe…`.
- Holdout: 36 fresh, seeded, off-lattice cases, sha256 `c7808c2f…`, seed 20261101. Each class gets one short case (S 384-896), one mid case (S 1408-5376) and one long case (S 12160 / 24576, b 1). Heads are odd counts: GQA 24/3, 40/8, 48/1, 96/4, 56/7; MHA 12/12, 20/20, 6/6. Ragged fills are 0.3 / 0.6 / 0.8. It was used once.

All 684 legs of the study ran, and so did the 12 spot-check legs below; none failed.

| generated (THD half, 8 classes) | 1.000 / 1.029 | 1.001 / 1.025 | 0 of 24 |

### Off the tile: `sm103_d256_bwd_thd_offtile_20261031`

These are the off-tile twins of the eight THD classes above.

- Train, 80 cases, sha256 `2ecd5adc…`. S is 200 / 1000 / 3000 / 9000 / 30000, every value off the 128 tile. Heads are GQA 16/2 and 8/1, MHA 16/16 and 6/6. b = round(tokens / S), with 32k tokens for the 16-head pairs and 8k for the small ones. Layouts are THD at equal lengths and half-to-full; masks are top-left causal and mask-free.
- Holdout: 24 seeded off-lattice cases, sha256 `a9ed2994…`, seed 20261104. Each class gets one short case (S 330 / 700), one mid case (S 2100 / 5000) and one long case (S 13000 / 21000, b 1). Heads are GQA 24/3, 40/8, 10/1, 12/2 and MHA 12/12, 5/5, 20/20. b is 1-12, and ragged fills are 0.3 / 0.6 / 0.8. It was used once.

All 312 legs ran; none failed.

| | train geomean / max regret | holdout geomean / max regret | holdout cases > 1.05 |
|---|---|---|---|
| generated (8 classes) | 1.000 / 1.012 | 1.000 / 1.000 | 0 of 24 |
| measured default | 1.307 / 1.875 | 1.298 / 1.687 | 23 of 24 |
| backend only | 1.307 / 1.873 | 1.295 / 1.683 | 23 of 24 |

**Dense half, not included.** Fitted on the same data, the four dense classes came out as one always and three `kv >= 7.5` thresholds. Three holdout cases failed:

- mask-free S 384, b 6, MHA 6/6: FROST 1.239x the backend;
- causal S 384, b 3, MHA 20/20: 1.088x;
- causal S 640, b 1, GQA 24/3: 1.05x.

The fit could not see this. At small S every dense training case had 16 heads, so the loss at small total work looked like a sequence-length boundary.

A follow-up study, `sm103_d256_bwd_v3_20261031`, refit the dense classes and their off-tile twins:

- Train: v2's dense records plus 96 new cells, sha256 `1f2723d8…`:
  - small work on the tile: S 128-640, b 1 / 4, 4-8 heads;
  - off the tile: S 200-30000.
- Holdout: 28 fresh cases, sha256 `ed647290…`.

It failed again. Holdout regret was 1.053 against the default's 1.153, but one case ran slower than the default: S 330, b 6, GQA 6/3, causal, off the tile, at 1.169x the default's time. The dense FROST/backend ratio is not monotone in any feature `gen.py` fits. It wins 0.71-0.78 at S 512-768 with few heads and loses 1.17-1.32 at S 330-700 with 3-6 heads. Dense entries therefore stay out until the class key can separate those regions.

**Backend version.** #2096's transfer check ran cuDNN 9.29.0.17, which is not on PyPI. The newest public wheel is `nvidia-cudnn-cu13 9.28.1.1.dev77966261`. Re-timing the backend leg alone on 12 holdout cases with it gave 0.997-1.019 of the 9.28.0.38 times, so the on-tile entries hold on that wheel; the off-tile study was not re-timed. Every grid and qualification uses 9.28.0.38.
