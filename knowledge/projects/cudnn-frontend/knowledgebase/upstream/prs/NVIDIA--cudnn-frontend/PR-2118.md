## Affected area

FE OSS kernels or CuTeDSL (SDPA forward default dispatch)

## Summary

Four default-dispatch gaps found by replaying the nightly results against develop's placement. Each has its own commit.

1. **Square causal masks get one verdict** (`fwd/placement.place`). A causal graph with `s_q == s_kv` covers the same tiles whether its diagonal is anchored top-left or bottom-right. `place()` now reads it as bottom-right, so the generated table and every hand rule treat both alignments alike.
   - Before: the paged d512 / d256 context entries, fitted on bottom-right chunks, never reached top-left full prefills, and those stayed on the backend while FROST was 1.4-2.9x faster on B200 / GB300.
   - The change exposed two hand rules that disagreed on the same computation: #1809 leads bottom-right paged d128 GQA4/8 from Q = 512 on cc 10.0 / 10.3; #1849 led top-left squares only from 1024, on cc 10.0, page 16.
   - A frozen 80-square study of the disputed cells (S 512-8192, page 16 / 64, b 1 / 3, cuDNN 9.29.0.18) ran FROST at 0.45-0.98x of the backend on a B100 and 0.49-0.99x on a B300. #1809's box stands, and #1849's test now expects those squares to lead.
2. **cc 10.7 dense half D64 proposes its native packed GQA set** (`fwd/heuristics`). Since #2053 an unsplit D64 graph selects the native D64 tile. `_pack_gqa_eligible` still admitted cc 10.7 dense packing only through the D128 shared leg, so no packed set was proposed and sink decode fell to an unpacked plan.
   - The gate now also reads `config_sm107.supports_native_d64_pack`, the predicate `mismatch()` already uses.
   - `_pack_gqa_points` keeps the unpacked set first while the unpacked launch fits one wave and there is no window. There every CTA streams the whole KV either way, and the packed CTA also computes the group's rows. Packed leads past one wave or under a window.
   - Qualified by a study on the target part and a fresh holdout that straddles the wave boundary (private study record).
3. **`benchmark/placement_gen` and the table key**:
   - The inference adapter takes the head pair and mask (causal / none) per case.
   - `fit()` no longer gives up a threshold's far-side wins for one marginal loss: `always` / `never` replace the threshold when every loss they accept is within twice `--margin` and their worst cell is lower.
   - `FEATURES` gains `q` (query tokens per request) for classes that hold decode-shaped and prefill graphs.
   - `class_key` labels a square causal mask bottom-right, so generator and runtime agree. Review found that a training-adapter study would otherwise emit top-left keys the runtime never looks up.
   - Replaying every stored study reproduces today's entries; only the unemitted `sm100_bwd_d256` study's labels move.
4. **cc 10.3 and cc 10.0 sliding-window context** (16 + 16 generated entries, studies `sm103_swa_prefill_20261031` on a B300 and `sm100_swa_prefill_20261031` on a B100, same frozen grid). The hand rules trailed every window graph off exact d512, based on a 2026-10-05 measurement. On a B300 with cuDNN 9.29.0.18:
   - FROST ran at 0.48-1.04x of the backend over 320 training cells (d64 with and without a sink, d128 / d256; dense and paged; windows 128 / 4096).
   - Generated placement scores regret 1.000 on 64 random holdout cells and 1.001 (max 1.024) on 32 on-tile ones, against 1.60 / 1.38 for develop's backend-first order.

5. **A100 (SM80) dense prefill** (36 generated entries, study `sm80_dense_prefill_20261031`, A100 80GB PCIe, cuDNN 9.29.0.18):
   - Coverage: d64 / d128 / d192x128 / d256; causal, mask-free, and d64 sink / window + sink; s_q 8-8192 over S_kv on and off the tile.
   - FROST now leads d192 and d256 prefill and most d64 graphs past a q / tiles threshold. Decode-shaped queries stay on the backend.
   - Two classes keep the hand order: d128 GQA causal (non-monotone) and d256 mask-free MHA (failed the final holdout).
   - The study took three rounds. Each holdout found an axis the grid had not crossed: first small s_q, then small s_q off the tile.
   - Final holdout (96 fresh cells): 0.76x of develop's time, no cell slower beyond noise.
   - `propose()` used to put trail-only rows after the last-resort marker regardless of `place()`. A trail-only row now leads where `place()` does, which only a generated entry can produce.
6. **cc 10.7 dense d64 decode / MTP and d256 MTP** (10 generated entries, study `sm107_dense_decode_20261031`, cuDNN 9.29.0.18, FROST timed with item 2 applied):
   - Since #2053 the cc 10.7 sink-decode arm, keyed on the D128 shared leg, no longer covered d64, so gpt-oss MTP had fallen back to the backend.
   - MTP now leads FROST at d64 (every gpt-oss mask) and d256. d64 single-row decode without a sink keeps the backend.
   - Qualified on a frozen 52-cell holdout off the training lattice (groups 5 / 6 / 12).
   - d256 single-row decode was fitted but left out: its frontier moves with the group, which needs a group axis first.
7. A cc 10.7 paged d256 decode / MTP table was added and then backed out within this PR. Its class-wide threshold contradicted the per-group frontiers the existing hand rule and its tests pin, and it extended to group 32 / page 32, which the study did not measure. It returns once a study covers the group axis.

## Why

The 2026-10-31 nightly showed these as the largest remaining default-dispatch regrets on Blackwell. Replaying that nightly out of sample through this branch moves 94 defaults on GB200 (geomean 0.74x of the old time, none slower than 1.007x) 94 on GB300 (0.78x; two gpt-oss sliding-window chunks at 1.031x / 1.037x, the rest faster or neutral) 135 on A100 (0.60x, none slower) and 49 on cc 10.7. The cc 10.7 entries are qualified by their studies rather than by this replay, because the nightly's FROST timings predate item 2. Item 2 changes FROST's own plan order, which the nightly's `cudnn_oss` leg cannot replay; it was measured directly instead.

On cc 10.0, the B100 SWA study re-timed 90 training cells that ran while another user's process held the GPU (32 had moved more than 5%) before fitting. Its holdout scores regret 1.000 (max 1.007), 0.63x of develop's order.
