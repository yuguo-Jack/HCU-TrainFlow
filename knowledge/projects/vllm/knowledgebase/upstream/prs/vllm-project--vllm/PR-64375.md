# [ROCm][RDNA][Perf] W4A16: fused scale/zero-point carrier for Triton prefill

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/64375) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description

## Overview

For asymmetric fp16 W4A16 layers on gfx1151 (Strix Halo), `RDNAHybridW4A16LinearKernel` now trades a little weight memory for faster prefill. At load time, each per-group scale and zero-point is packed into one int32 "carrier". The packed fp16 Triton prefill dequant (#60644) then does one load per (n, group) and a single `v_pk_fma_f16` per pair. Before, it did a scale load, a packed zero-point gather, and a subtract-then-multiply. With the cheaper dequant, asymmetric layers take the packed path from M > 192 (M > 128 when N ≥ 4K) instead of M > 256.

Builds on #60644. Not a duplicate: no open PR adds a scale/zero-point carrier or changes the asymmetric prefill cutoff. #61193 also speeds up gfx1151 prefill, but at M ≥ 4096 by dequantizing to a dense matmul; the two are complementary but touch the same file.

## Claims

- **Batch-1 prefill up to 18% faster end to end.** Qwen3-8B (asymmetric g128): −18.4% at 224 prompt tokens, −17.3% at 256. Qwen3-1.7B (asymmetric g64): −12.1% at 224, −12.4% at 256. No change at 128 tokens, and within ±1.8% from 320 tokens up.
- **No regression at batch 128 and 256** (outputs 1 and 128): Qwen3-8B is 3–11% faster, Qwen3-1.7B 1–5%.
- **Kernel** (geomean over 12 Qwen3 shapes, g128 and g64): 15–18% faster at M 193–256, 4–5% at M 129–192, 1–3% at M 257–4096. At M = 8192, summed over each model's four layers: 12% faster on Qwen3-8B, 5% on Qwen3-1.7B.
- **Memory cost:** 8/G of the int4 weight bytes (6.25% at g128, 12.5% at g64). Measured KV cache is 0.16–0.20% smaller.
- **Accuracy:** GSM8K within one question on both models. Symmetric output is bit-identical to main.
- **Unchanged:** symmetric layers, bf16, g32 (bit-identical to main), the skinny HIP decode path, and every GPU other than gfx1151.

## Details

**Carrier layout.** `[N, K//G]` int32: low 16 bits are the fp16 scale, high 16 bits are fp16 `bias_eff = -zp * scale`. Dequant is `(b_raw - 1024) * scale + bias_eff` on the magic-constant unpack from #60644. The output is not bit-identical to the zero-point path, because `bias_eff` is rounded to fp16 and fused into an FMA (see Accuracy).

**Where it is built.** Only asymmetric layers with fp16 scales, g ≥ 64, on gfx1151, the only place the packed dequant runs. On the scalar dequant (fp16 at small M, all bf16) the carrier measured about 1% slower on average with some cells 10–20% slower, since that path is VALU-issue-bound. It is a `Parameter`, so layerwise `reload_weights` recomputes it and copies it into its original storage. A non-persistent buffer would keep its stale value; the new reload test fails with one.

**Why the cutoffs.** Packed tiles with the carrier vs the scalar ladder (time ratio, < 1 favors the carrier), geomean over shapes. Shapes include synthetic K=4096 with N = 3K, 4K, 5K, since no real shape falls between N/K 2.4 and 6.

| g128 | 64 | 96 | 128 | 129 | 160 | 192 | 193 | 224 | 256 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| N < 4K (10 shapes) | 1.21 | 1.06 | 1.08 | 1.06 | 1.05 | 1.05 | 0.80 | 0.81 | 0.82 |
| N ≥ 4K (5 shapes) | 1.52 | 1.18 | 1.15 | 0.82 | 0.83 | 0.83 | 0.83 | 0.83 | 0.85 |

- M ≤ 128: the packed path loses on every shape, so it is unchanged.
- M 129–192: N ≥ 4K wins by 16–18%, including the synthetic N = 4K and 5K shapes.
- M > 192: everything wins. The step at 193 is the scalar ladder's 64-row tiles needing a fourth M-tile.
- From M 129 up, g64 matches g128 within 0.02.
- The `N ≥ 4K` rule is conservative: synthetic N = 3K gains 7% at M 129–192 and Qwen3 qkv shapes (N ≥ 6144, N/K 1.5–2.4) gain 6–11%. Every other shape with N ≤ 4096 loses there except Qwen3-4B o_proj (+2–3%). `N > 4096` would fit these 15 shapes better, but I kept the rule that holds without tuning to them.

**Why not g32.** At g32 the carrier would be 12–15% faster than main at M 193–256. From M 257, where main uses the zero-point gather, it is −3% to +2% against it (worst shape +20%), and it costs 25% of the int4 weight bytes. g32 keeps main's path: packed zero-point gather from M > 256.

**Memory.** Decode (`wvSplitK_int4_g`, #57199) still reads the original scales and zero-points, so the carrier is an extra copy. If decode read the carrier instead, those copies could be dropped; that is left for a follow-up.

**Interactions.**
- #60710's opt-in dense weight cache already covers M ≥ 256 when enabled, so with it on the carrier matters only at M 129–256.
- #52637 (open) also changes `process_weights_after_loading` in this file.
- #61193 (open) also edits the prefill dispatch in `rdna_hybrid_w4a16.py`.

I found two existing tests that aren't a great fit:
- `test_reload_weights` (`tests/model_executor/model_loader/test_reload.py`) uses Qwen3-0.6B-W4A16-G128, which is symmetric, so no carrier is built. Its few-token prompts also stay on the HIP decode path, which never reads the carrier.

I am having trouble finding a compelling use-case for reloading for these quantized weights, but it is allowed by the interface. The test itself takes about 1 second on a gfx1151 so I'd feel more comfortable leaving it in since it is fast.

Searching through for these tests also identified a bug. There is both layerwise and direct reloading. direct wasn't handled properly before so I've made that change and parameterized the test on that.
