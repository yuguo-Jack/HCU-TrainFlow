# [Perf] Pick the SM90 block-FP8 CUTLASS kernel by M size, not M % 4

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/60464) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description



## Overview

On Hopper, the CUTLASS block-FP8 GEMM picks between its normal and swap-AB kernels by whether M is a multiple of 4, which sends small aligned batches and large misaligned prefill chunks to the slower kernel. This PR picks by size instead (swap-AB up to M = 64) and pads the activation scales for misaligned larger M. Related: #60438 (DeepGEMM nvcc fallback).

## Claims

- Serving Qwen3-8B-FP8 on an H100 NVL with the CUTLASS path: 17 to 52% more output throughput.
- Decode step at batch 8: 9.30 to 6.64 ms. Batch 1 is unchanged.
- No change in accuracy (GSM8K 190 and 192 of 200) and no new host sync.

## Validation

H100 NVL, vLLM main `84bcbc6` (precompiled) with the rebuilt `_C_stable_libtorch`, Qwen3-8B-FP8, `VLLM_USE_DEEP_GEMM=0`, all arms in one session. DeepGEMM is shown for reference: where it can run, vLLM already prefers it.

<summary>GEMM sweep and tests</summary>

4 Qwen3-8B shapes x 33 values of M: total 60.8 to 19.9 ms. M a multiple of 4 up to 64: 1.6 to 1.9x faster. Misaligned large M: gate_up at M = 4097 10.5 to 1.27 ms, 8193 23.5 to 3.0 ms. Aligned large M within 6%.

`pytest tests/kernels/quantization/test_cutlass_scaled_mm.py -k blockwise`: 25/25 on the H100, including the two new misaligned shapes (M = 65 and 4097). They also pass on stock: the old path was slow, not wrong.

</details>

## Details

The SM90 TMA mainloop needs the M extent of the activation scales (SFA) to be a multiple of 4, so the dispatch used swap-AB exactly when M was not. Timing both kernels per M showed swap-AB is faster up to M = 64 and slower above. Now swap-AB is used for M up to 64, and for larger M that is not a multiple of 4 the per-token activation scales are padded to the next multiple of 4 and `layout_SFA` is built from the padded extent, as the SM100 caller already does.

Tradeoff: misaligned M above 64 now costs one extra allocation and an async device copy of the activation scales, which are 1/64 the size of the bf16 activation. Measured on one GPU (H100 NVL) and one model; DeepGEMM remains faster where it runs, so this matters when vLLM uses CUTLASS on Hopper: `VLLM_USE_DEEP_GEMM=0`, or a DeepGEMM that cannot JIT (#60438).

This is the SM90 counterpart of #57377 (SM100 scale padding) and #52775 (SM120 dispatch by M). If #58506 lands, SM90 can drop the scale copy the same way.
