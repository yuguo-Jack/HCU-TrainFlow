# [Bugfix][KV Offload][P2P] Keep store pins and AbortAck until NIXL transfers stop

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/59790) · state: closed · merged: True

## Original PR description

## Overview

The P2P producer released store pins, and sent `AbortAck` to the consumer, while its NIXL WRITEs could still be running. Neither cancelling the handle nor removing the remote peer stops a running WRITE with the UCX backend. This PR keeps the pins, and holds back `AbortAck`, until NIXL reports each transfer terminal. Addresses #59569 and the review on this PR. The consumer-side counterpart is tracked in #61532.

## Claims

- `NixlTransport.cancel(mode="wait")` releases only transfers that are no longer `PROC`/`PEND`. Previously it reported running WRITEs as cancelled, because `release_xfer_handle` never fails for an in-progress UCX request.
- A round whose store timed out, or which the consumer aborted, fails its jobs but keeps the pins of jobs that inflight transfers still read, until those transfers are terminal. Late completions settle each job exactly once, and new supply cannot restart such a round.
- The abort drain no longer falls back to `cancel(mode="immediate")` + `AbortAck` after `_CANCEL_DRAIN_TIMEOUT_S`. Past the timeout it logs once and keeps retrying. `AbortAck` is sent only when no transfer of the round can still write to the consumer.
- Wait-mode cancel retries are paced to one attempt per round per 100 ms, shared by the store-timeout sweep and the abort drain.
- When one transfer of a round fails, the round's other transfers are no longer cancelled with `mode="immediate"`. The round is marked cancelled instead: their pins and the round's `TransferDoneMsg(success=False)` wait until they are terminal.
- `nixlRemoteDisconnectError` from `check_xfer_state`, which NIXL raises when the peer process is gone, is treated as a terminal failure, so a dead peer no longer pins its transfers until the session closes.

## Validation

Real NIXL 1.4.1, UCX over TCP, CPU DRAM. This is not RDMA hardware.

| scenario | result |
|---|---|
| 512 MiB WRITE, old `cancel(mode="wait")` | returned `[]` while `PROC` in 5/5 runs; all bytes landed afterwards |
| 512 MiB WRITE, new `cancel(mode="wait")` | returned the tid as still inflight in 5/5 runs; `poll()` reported DONE only after all 512 MiB landed |
| 512 MiB WRITE, `remove_remote_peer` | returned with 14–392 MB landed in 5/5 runs; the rest still landed, and the handle then raises `nixlNotFoundError` |
| 1 GiB WRITE stalled by SIGSTOPping the target, then `cancel(mode="immediate")` + `remove_remote_peer`, then resume | 64/64 blocks still landed |
| stalled WRITE, target left stopped | stays `PROC` for 60 s, no timeout of its own |
| stalled WRITE, target SIGKILLed | `check_xfer_state` raises `nixlRemoteDisconnectError` |

## Details

**Root cause.** `NixlTransport.cancel(mode="wait")` relied on `release_xfer_handle` raising for a transfer that is still running. With UCX it never raises: `nixlUcxBackendReqH::release` calls `ucp_request_cancel`, which does nothing for an RMA PUT, then frees the request and returns success. The producer therefore treated running WRITEs as cancelled, released their store pins, and acked aborts while bytes were still landing. `remove_remote_peer` → `invalidateRemoteMD` only drops the connection entry, and the endpoint is later closed in flush mode, so outstanding writes complete rather than stop. That rules out the suggested "tear down the peer, then ack" fallback.

**Abort flow after this change.**
1. The consumer sends `AbortFetchMsg`.
2. The producer marks the round cancelled, fails the jobs no transfer reads, and keeps the rest pinned. A failed transfer takes its round down the same path.
3. It retries `cancel(mode="wait")` at the paced rate. A transfer leaves only when NIXL reports it terminal, or the peer is gone.
4. When no transfer of the round remains, the producer releases the remaining pins and sends `AbortAckMsg`. An aborted round sends no `TransferDoneMsg`.

**Tradeoff.** There is no hard deadline on the producer side. If a peer stays alive but the WRITE never progresses, the pins stay held, with one warning after `_CANCEL_DRAIN_TIMEOUT_S`. A bound would need a transport primitive that guarantees a running WRITE has stopped. An awaited forced endpoint close (`UCP_EP_CLOSE_FLAG_FORCE`) might be one, but NIXL does not expose it. The consumer still gives up after `_ABORT_ACK_TIMEOUT_S` and frees its slots; that is the open issue #61532.

The `store_timed_out` flag on a round is renamed `cancelled`, because it now covers aborts and failed transfers as well. All three paths share it: jobs no transfer reads fail at once, and the others keep their pins until their transfers are terminal.

The remote-disconnect check matches the binding's class name or the `NIXL_ERR_REMOTE_DISCONNECT` status code, so the NIXL bindings are not imported in this module. An error it does not recognize keeps the transfer inflight, which is the conservative side.

The shutdown drain in `manager.py` still falls back to `mode="immediate"` after `_SHUTDOWN_DRAIN_TIMEOUT_S`, because the engine is exiting. Only its docstring is updated.

This is distinct from #59329, which withdraws timed-out parked supply before Fetch. Its withdrawal set must be derived from the results actually emitted (`{r.job_id for r in results}`), so that unconfirmed inflight jobs are not removed from the round's pending jobs. Whichever PR merges second needs that adjustment.

1. **Liveness of a stuck transfer.** Before this change, the 30 s store timeout was an (unsafe) backstop: it eventually released the pins even if the transfer never finished. With the `continue`, an inflight job is only settled by `transport.poll()` reporting it done or failed. `NixlTransport.poll` reports failure only when `check_xfer_state` returns something other than `DONE`/`PROC`/`PEND`. If a transfer stays in `PROC` (a peer that died mid-write, or a hung RDMA path), nothing ever settles the job: the primary slots stay pinned and the round never finalizes. Could the timeout cancel the transfer instead of skipping it? For example, `self._transport.cancel([tid], mode="wait")` and then fail the round, the first option in #59569. With `mode="wait"` the handle is released only after NIXL confirms the abort, so the slots still aren't freed under a running write, and the job gets a bounded lifetime. If you'd rather rely on a transport-level deadline, a test showing what bounds a stuck `PROC` transfer would help.

2. **Interaction with #59329.** #59329 adds a pass right after this loop that withdraws the timed-out jobs from each round's `available` and `pending_job_ids`, using `set(timed_out)`. With your `continue`, `timed_out` would also contain the skipped inflight jobs, and withdrawing those from `pending_job_ids` while their transfer is still running would be wrong. 3. **Tests.** The two removed tests (`..._late_completion_no_duplicate` / `..._late_failure_no_duplicate`) covered "timed out, then the transport reports". That sequence can't happen for an inflight job anymore, so dropping them makes sense. A short note in the description saying so would help reviewers. The new parametrized test reads well. One more case worth adding is "inflight job past the deadline and the transfer never completes", which pins down the behaviour in point 1.

- apex-mochen · comment · 2026-10-14T10:08:36Z · [source](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/59790#issuecomment-6165938681)

Thanks @liranschour. Both points are addressed as of 84ec0d792f (changes in a975170245 and 4067dd2504, then a merge of main). Before getting to the bounded wait, one finding changes what a safe fallback can look like.

**`remove_remote_peer` does not stop a stalled write. Neither does a "confirmed" `cancel(mode="wait")`.** I checked the NIXL 1.4.1 and UCX 1.19 sources, then measured with real NIXL:

- `release_xfer_handle` on an in-progress UCX transfer calls `ucp_request_cancel` and then `ucp_request_free`, and returns success (`nixlUcxBackendReqH::release`, which has a TODO: "it may not be enough to cancel UCX request"). `ucp_request_cancel` only acts on tag-receive requests and does nothing for an RMA PUT. So the old `cancel(mode="wait")` never saw an exception and reported running writes as cancelled.
- `remove_remote_agent` calls `invalidateRemoteMD`, which calls UCX `disconnect`. That only erases the connection-map entry. In-flight requests still hold a `shared_ptr` to the connection, and the endpoint is later closed with `ucp_ep_close_nbx(flags=0)`: flush mode, non-blocking, with the close request freed immediately. Outstanding writes are flushed to completion rather than cancelled, and nothing reports when that happens.
- Real NIXL 1.4.1, UCX over TCP, CPU DRAM (not RDMA hardware):
  - One 512 MiB WRITE, 5/5 runs: `cancel(mode="wait")` returned `[]` while the transfer was still `PROC`, and all 512 MiB landed afterwards. In another 5/5 runs, `remove_remote_peer` returned with 14 to 392 MB landed, and the rest still landed.
  - A genuinely stalled 1 GiB WRITE (the target process SIGSTOPped, then resumed): after `cancel(mode="immediate")` + `remove_remote_peer`, all 64/64 blocks still landed once the target resumed. After `remove_remote_peer`, `check_xfer_state` raises `nixlNotFoundError`, so the transfer can no longer be observed. While the target stays stopped, the transfer stays `PROC` for at least 60 s and never times out on its own.

So a teardown followed by `abort_ack` would tell the consumer to reuse slots that can still be written. I have not added that fallback.

**Changes in this revision**

1. `NixlTransport.cancel(mode="wait")` checks the transfer state first. It releases only transfers that are no longer `PROC`/`PEND`, and keeps a transfer whose state check fails. Running transfers are returned as still inflight and surface later through `poll()`. Same 512 MiB setup: in 5/5 runs the tid comes back as still inflight, and `poll()` reports DONE only after all 512 MiB have landed.
2. Backoff: each round gets at most one wait-mode cancel attempt per 100 ms (`last_cancel_at` on the round). The store-timeout sweep and the abort drain share this limit, so a round that is both expired and aborted gets one attempt per interval, not one per path per tick.
3. The abort drain no longer has a timed fallback. Previously, for rounds whose store had not timed out, it ran `cancel(mode="immediate")` after `_CANCEL_DRAIN_TIMEOUT_S` and sent `AbortAck` anyway. Given the above, that ack could let the consumer reuse slots that are still being written. Now `AbortAck` is sent only once NIXL reports every transfer of the round terminal. Past the timeout the drain logs one warning and keeps retrying at the paced rate. An aborted round also keeps the pins of the jobs its inflight transfers are reading, the same way a store-timed-out round does. The flag `store_timed_out` is renamed to `cancelled` to cover both. Jobs that no transfer reads still fail right away, and the aborted round sends no `TransferDoneMsg`.
4. A dead peer now ends its transfers. When the target process dies, `check_xfer_state` raises `nixlRemoteDisconnectError` instead of returning `"ERR"`; I measured this with a stalled WRITE and a SIGKILLed target over UCX/TCP. Previously `poll()` and `cancel(mode="wait")` treated any state-check exception as still running, so a dead peer pinned its transfers until the session closed. That specific exception is now terminal (failed); other exceptions still keep the transfer.

**Bounded wait.** The consumer does not wait forever: `client.py` fails the load after `_ABORT_ACK_TIMEOUT_S` (10 s) even without an ack, and existing tests cover this. The wait that stays unbounded is the producer holding pins for a write that is stalled but whose peer is still alive. I don't see a NIXL primitive that guarantees a running WRITE has stopped. A forced endpoint close (`UCP_EP_CLOSE_FLAG_FORCE`) whose completion is awaited might be one, but NIXL doesn't expose it. If the peer dies, (4) now releases the pins.

The consumer side is still open: after its own 10 s, the consumer reuses its slots without an ack, and a late write can land in them. Fixing that needs the stop primitive above or a consumer-side quarantine, so I kept it out of this PR. It is tracked in #61532.

- Transport:
  - A wait-mode cancel keeps `PROC`/`PEND` transfers, and a failed state check also keeps them.
  - `nixlRemoteDisconnectError` is reported as failed by `poll()` and dropped by `cancel(mode="wait")`.
  - Terminal transfers are released.
- Session:
  - Retry pacing works for both the store sweep and the abort drain.
  - An unconfirmed cancellation never releases the pin, sends `AbortAck` or `TransferDone`, force-cancels, or removes the peer, both for a store-timed-out round over 500 s of simulated time and for a plain aborted round past `_CANCEL_DRAIN_TIMEOUT_S`.
  - An abort fails the jobs no transfer reads at once and keeps the others pinned until the transfer stops.
  - A transfer that finishes on its own after the abort (done or failed) releases its pin and lets the abort ack, with no `TransferDone`.
- The 6 behavior tests from the previous step, and the 6 added in this step, fail on the respective previous logic and pass now.
