# [ROCm][Perf] Use AITER FlyDSL INT4 QuickReduce when INT4 QuickReduce is selected

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/59478) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description


<!-- markdownlint-disable -->

## Overview

Whenever INT4 QuickReduce is selected (`VLLM_ROCM_QUICK_REDUCE_QUANTIZATION=INT4`) on gfx942/gfx950 with AITER enabled, vLLM creates AITER's FlyDSL INT4 QuickReduce communicator (`aiter.ops.flydsl.QuickAllReduceInt4`, [ROCm/aiter#4970](https://github.com/ROCm/aiter/pull/4970), merged; TP 2/4/8) next to the HIP one. A bf16, contiguous, 16-byte-aligned payload that HIP QuickReduce would take at the INT4 level runs FlyDSL instead; every other payload runs HIP QuickReduce as before. FlyDSL has no fused RMSNorm kernel yet, so with the fused all-reduce + RMSNorm pass on, such a payload takes FlyDSL all-reduce plus a separate RMSNorm, and the pass is a no-op for it. vLLM warns once when `fuse_allreduce_rms` is on. There is no new environment variable, and QuickReduce itself stays off unless a codec is selected. AITER measures the kernel 29–47% faster than HIP INT4 at TP8 (512 × 4096: 18.1 vs 26.5 µs; 32768 × 5120: 558 vs 1061 µs).

Not a duplicate: searched open and closed PRs for "FlyDSL QuickReduce". [#61021](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/61021) and [#61020](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/61020) add RMSNorm and MXFP4 epilogues to this kernel and build on it.

## Claims

- bf16 payloads inside the HIP QuickReduce window run 1.2–1.5× faster than the HIP kernel on the same payload (TP4, real kernels). With the fused all-reduce + RMSNorm pass on, FlyDSL all-reduce plus a separate RMSNorm is no slower than the fused-pass route the nightly takes for the same payload, 1.05–2.96× faster at TP2, TP4 and TP8 (table below). fp16 and other payloads run HIP QuickReduce at HIP speed.
- On Qwen3.8-2.4T, TP8, MI355X, serving throughput with this PR is 1.044× stock (geomean of nine cells, ahead in 16 of 18 seed pairs; +4–6% in most prefill-heavy cells, +2% at 1024 / 1024 c256, flat at c128), and median TPOT is 4–6% lower in six of the nine cells. GSM8K is unchanged (0.9726 stock, 0.9726 and 0.9749 with the PR).

## Details

- **Routing.** HIP QuickReduce is always initialized and its size window applies first (at least 2 MiB at TP4 and TP8, at least 1 MiB at TP2; 128 tokens at hidden 8192 is 2 MiB). FlyDSL runs a payload that is bf16, contiguous, 16-byte-aligned and at the INT4 level for its size (below `VLLM_ROCM_QUICK_REDUCE_QUANTIZATION_MIN_SIZE_KB` the FP codec runs). There is no FlyDSL size floor of its own: below the HIP window it measured 14% slower than the custom-AR kernel at 0.5 MiB on TP8, so the window must not be widened. A decode batch of 128 tokens or more at hidden 8192 is inside the window and runs FlyDSL; smaller decode payloads stay on the custom-AR kernels, which this PR does not change.
- **Fused all-reduce + RMSNorm.** A payload FlyDSL owns skips the fused HIP QR+RMSNorm and the fused custom-AR RMSNorm kernels and runs all-reduce plus a separate RMSNorm, so `fuse_allreduce_rms` does nothing for it, and vLLM warns once when the pass is on. This is not slower in the measured window: the FlyDSL all-reduce is faster than the HIP kernel on every payload at TP4 and TP8 and within 0.3% at TP2 (32 MiB), and FlyDSL plus a separate RMSNorm is at worst 1.07× slower than the HIP kernel with the fused RMSNorm applied by hand (TP2 at 32 MiB; at TP4 and TP8 it ties or wins), a route the nightly does not take (next bullet). Fusing RMSNorm into the FlyDSL kernel is [#61021](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/61021) and the MXFP4 epilogue is [#61020](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/61020); both are prefill fusions and need [ROCm/aiter#6369](https://github.com/ROCm/aiter/pull/6369).
- **The fused HIP QR+RMSNorm did not fire in the image checked.** In `nightly-3f1bcfd8` (AITER 0.1.25) and `nightly-5555a2d8` (AITER 0.1.24.post1), `_get_or_create_aiter_qr_rmsnorm_comm` fails on `aiter.qr_get_handle(ptr)`: AITER's signature is `qr_get_handle(fa, out_ptr)` since [ROCm/aiter#4421](https://github.com/ROCm/aiter/pull/4421), the call raises `TypeError`, and the bare `except Exception` returns `None`. In both images the function returned `None` for vLLM's own communicator (TP2 on `5555a2d8`, TP2, TP4 and TP8 on `3f1bcfd8`). Fused all-reduces there ran the custom-AR kernel instead. The control image of the earlier A/B (`nightly-92044241`) was not re-checked. This PR does not touch that function.
- **Architecture gate.** The communicator is created when `get_cdna_version() in (3, 4)`: AITER's `QuickAllReduceInt4` supports gfx942 and gfx950 only. It runs AITER kernels, so `VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER` is the switch that turns it off.
- **Limitations and CI gaps.** The serving A/B has one run of the stock arm and no A/A control. The measured uplift comes from payloads of 2 MiB and up, which only the prefill cells and decode batches of 128 tokens or more reach; at hidden 8192 the kernel sweep predicts the size: FlyDSL saves about 180 µs per all-reduce on a 128 MiB payload at TP8, about 6% of an 8192-token prefill pass over 184 all-reduces, and the 8k / 128 c16 cell measured +6%. The multi-GPU QuickReduce tests skip in CI without GPUs.

- maeehart · comment · 2026-10-07T15:41:44Z · [source](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/59478#issuecomment-6041349828)

**MI325X (gfx942), DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at TP2, TP4 and TP8, and NIAH**

@mjkvaak-amd, I ran the same A/B on MI325X with `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` at TP2, TP4 and TP8, in the same image with this PR applied at `7d4a0975`. With the flag set, every rank logged `Using AITER FlyDSL INT4 QuickReduce for BF16 payloads from 2 MiB through 2048 MiB`, and without it no rank did. All servers ran without errors, and every request completed.

The gain grows with the TP size. At TP8, FlyDSL lowers the mean TTFT geomean by 10.5%, with 6.5 to 19.8% per cell, and raises output throughput by 9.1%, which is close to the −11.3% of your MI355X TP8 table. At TP4 the geomean is −1.9%. The four TP4 cells with a spread below 0.2% over seeds (8k at concurrency 4, and 32k, 60k and 128k) are 1.0 to 1.4% lower, and the cells at concurrency 16 and 64 move within their spread. At TP2 the geomean is +0.0%, because the 2k cell at concurrency 16 varies by about 10% between seeds, while every TP2 cell from 8k up is 0.7 to 2.0% lower.

I think that the gain is largest at TP8 because each rank computes only an eighth of every layer there, so the all-reduce takes a larger part of each prefill step. Also, at TP2 HIP QuickReduce INT4 starts at 1 MiB instead of 2 MiB, so steps of about 103 to 204 tokens use HIP QuickReduce in the control and the custom all-reduce with FlyDSL, because this PR keeps the 2 MiB floor at every TP size.

Decode does not change at any TP size, with median ITL within 0.5%, because one token of this model is 10 KiB, so decode all-reduces of up to 64 tokens stay below the 2 MiB floor and go to the custom all-reduce in both arms. Mean TPOT is still 6.7% lower at TP8, because TPOT also counts the steps where decodes run together with a prefill chunk, and those steps are shorter with FlyDSL.

`DeepseekV41ForCausalLM` is not torch-compiled in this image, and vLLM logs that the model does not support `torch.compile`, so `fuse_allreduce_rms` has no effect on it. The two arms therefore differ only in the QuickReduce kernel, and these numbers do not show what FlyDSL costs on a model where the all-reduce and RMSNorm fusion runs.

GSM8K does not show a loss. FlyDSL is 1.3 points higher at TP4, equal at TP2 and 1.1 points lower at TP8, and each difference is at most 1.3 standard errors of the difference, so I read them as run-to-run variation. No second `_ptr` is needed, and enabling FlyDSL does not break the fusion. The fused op never uses `QuickAllReduce._ptr`. `_rocm_aiter_fused_allreduce_rmsnorm_impl` creates its own AITER communicator (`_get_or_create_aiter_qr_rmsnorm_comm`) the first time it runs, and it asks `qr_comm` only two things: whether it is `disabled`, and whether `should_quick_allreduce(input_)` accepts the payload. Notice that FlyDSL QR doesn't yet support fusions with RMSNorm and/or Quant kernels.

My reasons for keeping this as opt-in for now:
