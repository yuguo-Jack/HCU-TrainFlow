# [ROCm][Perf][DSv4.1] Asm decode layers for 17-256-row decode steps (gfx950, TP4)

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/65421) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description



## Overview

Hand/Agent-written gfx950 kernels for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash decode at TP4, covering the decode steps of 17-256 rows
that the mono decode layer (`--monokernel`) leaves to vLLM's ops. All code is in
`vllm/models/deepseek_v41/amd/`, next to the mono decode layer.

## Claims

- 1.15-1.54× lower median TPOT than `--monokernel` alone at 8k and 200k context, c32-128 (MI355X × 4, TP4).
- 1.32× with DSpark (5 drafts) at 8k c16. Parity at c64, whose 384-row steps are past this PR's 256.
- No accuracy change: gsm8k, and needle retrieval up to 1M context, match.
- On automatically with `--monokernel` on gfx950 at TP4. No new flags or env vars; every other configuration is
  unchanged.

## Details

What runs where, for decode steps of 17-256 rows (steps up to 16 rows stay on the mono decode layer, whose limit
drops from 48 to 16 when this is on, because it is faster there):

- K1, the attention half: seam, front (wqkv, norms, SWA insert, wq_b), sparse attention, and back (wo_a, wo_b) with
  all-reduce #1 folded into wo_b's launch (MXFP8 wire). On the index layers it also covers the indexer's q side,
  the compressor tail, and the indexer's logits + top-512 in one launch, up to 32 · 16384 compressed positions.
  Wider rows use vLLM's indexer.
- K2, the FFN half: seam, router, shared expert, routed MoE (MXFP4 experts), combine with all-reduce #2 folded in.
- Engram: wkv moves to the prefetch stream (resharded once, so the kv all-gather goes away); the next attention
  seam applies gate · value.
- Prefill, mixed steps' attention and steps above 256 rows stay on vLLM's path.

Code: `asm/decode.py` holds `AsmDecodeLayer`, created next to `MonoDecodeLayer`. `model.py` calls into it at the
decoder layer's seams, attention and FFN. `rocm.py` routes the attention's eager break (`_sparse_indexer_and_attn`)
through it. `asm/engram.py` subclasses `Engram`, and `mono_decode.py` takes its row limit from it.
`asm/kernels/` are Python generators of AMDGPU assembly. They are assembled with the image's LLVM (clang, ld.lld)
on each worker's first forward, cached under `$VLLM_CACHE_ROOT/dsv41_asm`, and launched through HIP's module API
(ctypes). The generators carry `# ruff: noqa: E501` and `# mypy: ignore-errors`, as the Muse Spark monokernel's
do. `pyproject.toml`: typos allows `offen`, the buffer instructions' offset-enable modifier.

It turns on with `--monokernel` on gfx950 at TP4, with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's shapes and clang / ld.lld found.
It stays off, with a log line, for LoRA, KV transfer, pipeline or decode context parallelism, and per layer for
another KV cache dtype or MoE routing. Expert or data parallelism is already refused by `--monokernel`.

Limitations and tradeoffs:
- TP4 only: the folded all-reduces are written for 4 ranks. At most 256 rows per decode step.
- About 16k lines, nearly all generated-assembly sources. They are self-contained (no dependency changes), and no
  shared or core code path changes.
- The weights are repacked on the first forward, so in-place weight reloads aren't picked up.
- Numerics are not bitwise vLLM's (MXFP8 all-reduce wire, a different rounding order). They were checked against
  fp64 references on captured layers during development and end to end as above.
- CI: `tests/models/test_dsv41_asm_layers.py` joins the MI355 4-GPU DSv4.1 mono decode step (`test-amd.yaml`,
  `test_areas/models_basic.yaml`). It reuses `test_dsv41_mono_layers.py`'s five-layer random-weight model: TP4,
  32-row decode steps, `--monokernel` on (the asm path; a probe checks K1 / K2 / Engram took the steps on every
  rank) against off, each layer's outputs per token. Its bounds are wider than the mono test's because the
  all-reduces carry MXFP8 here. Skips off gfx950, under 4 GPUs, or without clang / ld.lld.

With this PR, a decoder layer on a 17–256-row decode step runs as a chain of roughly 10–15 launches:

 1. `k1_seam2q` (`k1_seam2fy` on index layers, `*_eg` after an Engram layer): attention seam, i.e. mHC post-mix of the previous FFN + pre-mix, Σx² partials, MXFP8 of x · attn_norm.w.
 2. `gemv_wqkv32n`: wqkv GEMV (q / kv rows), plus a wave finishing the seam's mixes (post / comb / pre, rstd).
 3. `wq_b` GEMV (`gemv8`, kv wave): wq_b + q RoPE, and the kv norm + RoPE + SWA cache insert in the same launch.
     - index layers: the `_x2` variant, which also runs the indexer's wq_b + q RoPE + MXFP4 quant as extra CTAs;
     - index layers, on aux streams in parallel: `comp_gemm` + `comp_tail` (Triton: compressor GEMM, pooling / norm, compressed KV insert, indexer K store) and vLLM's `weights_proj`.
 4. Index layers only, in the attention eager break: indexer logits + top-512, one of:
     - `idx_logits` (fused logits + top-512, one CTA per row), for short rows;
     - the wide split-logits kernel + its merge kernel, plus the top-2048 candidate-block selector on the candidate-source layer;
     - the gather-mode kernel, on candidate-consumer layers;
     - `_translate_kernel` (Triton) when vLLM's top-k ran instead (wider rows).
 5. `k1_attn3`: sparse attention (SWA + compressed top-k), in the attention eager break.
 6. `wo_a` GEMV (`gemv8`, k or t mode).
 7. `wob_ar`: wo_b GEMV with all-reduce #1 folded in (fp8 wire).
 8. `ffn_seam2_ug`: FFN seam (mHC post-mix of the attention + pre-mix, Σx² partials, MXFP8 + bf16 of x · ffn_norm.w).
 9. `ffn_rt_sgu`, two bodies merged into one launch (`multi.py`):
     - router logits partials (bf16 MFMA);
     - shared-expert gate_up partials (fp8 MFMA).
 10. `ffn_p3v3`: one CTA per token:
     - rstd, router finish (sqrt-softplus + bias, top-6, renormalise × 1.5);
     - shared-expert finish (SwiGLU, MXFP8);
     - a 5th wave finishing the FFN seam's mixes.
 11. `ffn_prep_sdn`, two bodies merged into one launch:
     - MoE prep (sort tokens by expert, per-expert counts / cost split);
     - shared-expert down GEMV.
 12. `moe_ug1`: routed-expert stage 1 (MXFP4 gate_up + SwiGLU).
 13. `moe_down1_table`: routed-expert stage 2 (MXFP4 down).
 14. `comb_ar`: MoE combine (weighted routed + shared) with all-reduce #2 folded in (fp8 wire).

That is 13 launches on a non-index layer, and 14–16 on an index layer (plus the two Triton compressor launches on aux streams). Dense / ratio-0 layers (e.g. layer 0) only take the K2 half (launches 8–14) and keep vLLM's attention. On Engram layers, the gate all-gather and the rows push / wait (`eg_ag`) are added.

The reason this is a single blob is because it has been optimized as a single blob, as in kernel boundaries have been chosen to be bespoke to DSv4.1 with no limitations in fusions and sizes are hardcoded, the kernels simply do not work over M 256 and exact DSv4.1 sizes. We could separate them and look for sharing but nowadays that actually takes more time and effort than just doing the bespoke part per model.

The reason the kernels are shipped like this is to both have generators and allow just python files in a PR. We could pregen them and ship even fully assembled versions but the benefits are questionable. And the kernels are for vLLM only. Shipping single model vLLM only kernels in AITER would not bring much benefit, and just introduce undue delay in shipping to users as one first needs to wait for AITER PR, then pinning new AITER to vLLM and then the final vLLM PR.

Assembly over FlyDSL allows more precise instruction scheduling. With FlyDSL one has to figure out how to get the FlyDSL generate the instructions you want, with direct assembly one can just put in what has been found to be efficient.

This sort of development does not make sense if there are humans in the loop. These are there result of agentic optimization loops bespoke for that single problem, so they are essentially black boxes where we have verified the numerical results of the kernels both individually and as part of real serving.
