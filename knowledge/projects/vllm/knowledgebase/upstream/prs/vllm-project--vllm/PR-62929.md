# [Bugfix][KV Offload] Hold promotions after a cache reset until jobs report

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/62929) · state: closed · merged: True

## Original PR description


## Overview

`reset_cache()` in the `OffloadingConnector` scheduler releases the CPU-tier chunks of in-flight transfer jobs at once, but the workers may still write those chunks until they report the jobs. With secondary tiers, a lookup in that window can promote a secondary-tier hit into one of the released chunks, and the late worker write then overwrites it, so a later load hands the GPU wrong KV. This PR adds an `OffloadingManager.on_reset_flushed()` hook, called once the workers have reported every job flushed by the reset, and `TieringOffloadingManager` holds promotions from `reset_cache()` until then.

It is a standalone bugfix on `main` and also applies to a single node.

## Claims

- With `TieringOffloadingSpec` and at least one secondary tier, no promotion into the CPU tier starts after a cache reset until the workers have reported every job the reset flushed. This covers lookups from the scheduler and from tiers that serve peers through their `ParentManager` (`p2p`, `kvcr`). A late DMA of a flushed store, or any other worker write still pending, can no longer land on a chunk a promotion has just filled.
- Lookups keep running in that window: CPU-tier hits are served as before, and a chunk found only in a secondary tier is a miss, so the request recomputes it (or the peer is told it is absent).
- Without secondary tiers (`CPUOffloadingSpec`, or `TieringOffloadingSpec` without tiers), nothing is held (see Details for why it is not needed there). The scheduler still waits for the flushed jobs' reports before calling the hook, so after a reset with jobs in flight the engine keeps stepping until they arrive, normally on the next step.
- While the scheduler waits, the engine keeps stepping even with no request left (`has_pending_push_work()`), so the flush reaches the workers and their reports come back on an idle engine too.
- The scheduler logs when it starts and stops waiting for the reports, and warns once if that lasts more than 60 s; the check runs on every step, including the empty steps of an idle engine.

## Details

**Root cause.** `reset_cache()`, reached through `reset_prefix_cache(reset_connector=True)` (`POST /reset_prefix_cache?reset_external=true`, or `pause_generation(clear_cache=True)`), adds every in-flight job to `jobs_to_flush`, resets the manager, which frees their CPU-tier chunks, and drops later worker reports of those jobs through `_stale_job_threshold`. The workers submit and wait for the flushed jobs in `handle_preemptions()` of the next step they run. The scheduler builds that same step first, and its lookups run before any worker has seen the flush. On a secondary-tier hit, `TieringOffloadingManager.lookup()` reserves a free CPU-tier chunk (`_initiate_promotion()` → `primary_tier.prepare_write()`), which may be one the reset has just released, and the tier copies into it from the engine core after `on_schedule_end()`. Nothing orders that copy against the worker's DMA into the same chunk. If the DMA lands last, the chunk holds stale KV (or KV from blocks the GPU already reused) under the promoted key, and the next load hands it to the GPU. `TieringOffloadingManager.reset_cache()` already drains the secondary tiers' own transfers for exactly this reason ("preferable to silent corruption from reusing primary slots while a transfer is mid-copy"); the workers' transfers were not covered.

The scheduler is not the only caller of `lookup()`. Tiers that serve external requests (`p2p`, `kvcr`) call it through their `ParentManager` from `serve_external_requests()`, in `on_schedule_end()` and on the tier-poll thread, and such a lookup promotes from any other secondary tier the same way.

**Why only promotions.** A promotion is the only write to a CPU-tier chunk that does not come from a worker. A worker submits the flushed stores and waits for them before it submits any job of a later step, and a load, or a secondary tier's read for a cascade or a peer, only uses a chunk whose post-reset store has completed. So a late worker write either finishes before anything that depends on the chunk or is overwritten by it. That is why lookups keep serving CPU-tier hits during the hold, and why engines without secondary tiers hold nothing.

**Fix.**

- `OffloadingManager.on_reset_flushed()` is new, a no-op by default: it is called once the workers have reported every job in flight at the last `reset_cache()`, so blocks that reset freed are no longer written by them.
- `TieringOffloadingManager.reset_cache()` holds promotions when it has secondary tiers, and `on_reset_flushed()` releases them. While held, its `lookup()` returns `MISS` for a chunk that is not in the CPU tier, without querying the secondary tiers, whoever calls it. `MISS` rather than `RETRY`: `RETRY` would stall the request (and keep a peer's lookup pending) until the hold is released, while the chunk can simply be recomputed. The hold sits in the manager rather than the scheduler because peers' lookups reach `lookup()` without going through the scheduler.
- At reset, if any job is in flight, the scheduler records, for every flushed job, the worker reports still due (`pending_count`, so reports received before the reset count). Stale reports decrement it as they arrive, and the last one calls `on_reset_flushed()`; with nothing in flight it is called right away. The reports normally arrive within a step or two of the reset, so the cost is a short window without promotions.
- `has_pending_push_work()` also returns true while the scheduler waits for those reports. The reset clears `_jobs`, so without this an engine left with no request went idle before the flush reached the workers, and promotions (including peers' lookups) stayed held until unrelated work arrived.

**Limitations.** A worker that never reports its flushed jobs keeps promotions held; the warning makes that visible.

**Not a duplicate.** I searched open PRs (`reset_cache offloading`, `offload promotion reset`, `TieringOffloadingManager reset_cache`, `hold promotions offload`). None holds promotions across a reset. The closest are complementary:

- #59099 keeps the engine stepping until a reset's flush reaches the workers and makes `reset_cache()` re-entrant. This PR keeps the engine stepping only until the flushed jobs report; #59099 also covers engines without secondary tiers. A second reset before they report adds its own flushed jobs to the wait.
- #61667 quarantines `p2p` consumer slots whose remote write is unconfirmed. It covers a secondary tier's own late write, not a worker's.

**Related.** The multi-node CPU-region mirror proposed in #54415 (#62933) adds a second late writer, a remote node's pending slot push, and relies on this hook to cover it.
