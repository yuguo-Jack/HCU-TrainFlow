- Aravind0403 · comment · 2026-11-04T04:59:05Z · [source](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/65580#issuecomment-6622976816)

Hi @hsthanb4, thanks for looking into this!

We tested this patch against the CPU reproducer from #65550 across $N \in [129, 176]$ ($W=128, B=16$), and the 3 lengths still fail with this change (3 failed, 45 passed):

```text
FAILED N=143: oldest=15, block=0, table=[0, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17]
FAILED N=159: oldest=31, block=1, table=[0, 0, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18]
FAILED N=175: oldest=47, block=2, table=[0, 0, 0, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19]
```
Why this happens:

Recomputation step has num_external_computed_tokens == 0: In Step 1 (initial async load), num_external_computed_tokens > 0, but once the transfer completes and the scheduler schedules the recomputation of token $N-1$ in Step 3, allocate_slots is called with num_new_tokens=1 and num_external_computed_tokens=0. Thus the if condition evaluates to False, remove_skipped_blocks(request_id, processed_tokens=N) runs in Step 3, and block $k-1$ is freed right before token $N-1$ executes attention.

Sender-side trimming: On the prefill instance, kv_cache_manager computes all $N$ tokens and trims block $k-1$ on the sender, so the prefiller does not retain or transfer block $k-1$ over the connector.

Connector-level backoff (_prefill_backoff = 1 for SlidingWindowSpec) ensures both sender and receiver agree to stop at $N-1$, keeping block $k-1$ intact throughout. We opened #65584 addressing both sides with full test coverage if you'd like to take a look!
