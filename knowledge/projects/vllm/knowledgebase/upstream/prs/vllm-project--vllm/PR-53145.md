# [Bugfix][Whisper] Recover audio abandoned inside a chunk

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/53145) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description

## Purpose

Fixes #53144 — Whisper transcription silently drops audio when the decoder stops early inside a chunk.

Each audio chunk is decoded exactly once. When the model emits end-of-transcript before the chunk it was given is exhausted, everything after its last timestamp is transcribed by nobody: `HTTP 200`, a `duration` covering the whole file, `text` that reads like a finished sentence, and no field anywhere saying something is missing.

It is not the chunking — it reproduces on a file **shorter than 30 s**, where no splitting happens at all.

## Not a duplicate

At the time this PR was opened there was no open PR for this. **#53236 was opened afterwards, for the same issue, and takes the same overall approach** (re-submit the abandoned tail). The differences are substantive rather than stylistic, and I would rather they be judged than quietly ignored:

| | this PR | #53236 |
|---|---|---|
| protection against invented text | speech-density test on recovered regions | none |
| response formats fixed | all (coverage read from token ids) | `verbose_json` only |
| gap threshold | 2 s | 0.5 s — fires on ordinary trailing silence |
| slice boundary | pre-roll + seam de-duplication | cut exactly at the last timestamp, mid-word |
| bounds | pass ceiling, error handling, log line when a stretch is left unrecovered | none |

The first row is the one I would ask a reviewer to weigh. An abandoned stretch is usually **silence**, and Whisper does not stay quiet over silence — measured on this deployment, 13.8 s of near-silence came back as `" Let's go."` with `avg_logprob -0.46`, a perfectly ordinary sentence that no blocklist would carry. Re-submitting without a guard trades a silent LOSS of speech for a silent INVENTION of it, which is worse: a user notices a missing tail, but not a fluent sentence in the middle of their transcript.

I have no stake in which branch lands. If the maintainers prefer #53236, the guards here can be folded into it, and I am happy to do that work.

## Measurements

`v0.27.1`, `openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo`, real recordings (the ruled-out hypotheses are in the issue):

| recording | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| 29.42 s, single chunk | text ends at **21.56 s** — 7.9 s of speech absent | ends at 28.80 s, 176 → **246** characters |
| 58.85 s, two chunks | tail of **both** windows lost — a third of the speech | 353 → **516** characters, 4 → 8 segments |
| speech + 12 s silence + speech | — | both halves present, nothing invented |

Coverage gap after the fix is ~0.6 s on all three — trailing silence, not speech.

## What the change does

After the per-chunk outputs are merged, each chunk's decoded coverage (from its own timestamp tokens) is compared against its length. A stretch the decoder walked away from is re-submitted as its own request and appended to the chunk it came from — so ordering needs no sorting: the abandoned stretch belongs at the end of its own chunk and before the next one, which is exactly where appending puts it.

Two details are deliberate:

- **Coverage is read from token ids, not logprobs.** `logprobs` are requested only for `verbose_json`, so reading them would leave `json` and `text` clients unfixed — and those are precisely the clients that cannot notice the loss themselves, having no segments to measure.
- **The phantom guard is speech density, not a phrase list**, for the reason given above. It is applied **only** to recovered regions, where the fallback is no text at all — exactly what the transcript had before this change. A false positive there costs nothing that was not already lost.

Everything is bounded: a minimum gap worth asking about, a pre-roll so the slice does not start mid-word (with the resulting repetition removed at the seam), a ceiling on recovery requests, and a log line whenever a stretch is left unrecovered — a silent stop would reintroduce the same defect one level deeper. Beam search is skipped rather than half-supported.

Two small observations:

1. The 3 words still missing on the 16-minute clip are in the last chunks. The decoder's own timestamp says 2.32 s were abandoned at the 957.68 s cut, but the energy-based chunk is a little shorter than 30 s, so on 0.25.1's `idx * chunk_size` offsets the stretch computes to just under `UNDECODED_MIN_GAP_S = 2.0` and is skipped. main's exact `chunk_start_offsets` should make that arithmetic tighter; a threshold around 1.5 s would also catch it without touching the speech-density guard.
2. The 0.5 s pre-roll shows up as recovered segments that start before the previous segment's end (e.g. 26.42 → 25.92). Harmless for text, but consumers that assume monotonic segments should know.

Happy to re-run on sm_120 after any revision.
