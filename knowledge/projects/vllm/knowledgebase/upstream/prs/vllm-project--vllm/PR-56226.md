# [ROCm][Perf] Route softmax MoE top-k, including fused shared-expert sigmoid, through AITER topk_gating

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/56226) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description

<!-- markdownlint-disable -->

## Overview

Blocked on [ROCm/aiter#6306](https://github.com/ROCm/aiter/pull/6306) (adds the shared-expert suffix to `topk_gating`): this stays a draft until that is merged and released.

On ROCm, softmax MoE routing uses AITER's legacy `topkGatingSoftmax` launcher. This routes `FusedTopKRouter` and the fused-shared-expert router (`VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER_FUSION_SHARED_EXPERTS=1`) through `aiter.ops.topk.topk_gating` instead, which keeps selected-mass renormalization in-kernel (ROCm/aiter#4460), has the E=512 `prefill_n` dispatch (ROCm/aiter#5334, merged), and with #6306 scores the shared-expert columns with sigmoid in the same launch. `dispatch_topk_softmax_func` decides per launch between `topk_gating` and the legacy `topk_softmax`. This is the default on gfx942 and gfx950 and there is no new env var; every other architecture keeps the legacy launcher. gfx950 is the main sweep; gfx942 (MI300-class) was verified more lightly (see the gfx942 section).

Not a duplicate: searched open PRs for `topk_gating` and `aiter topk softmax`; no other PR routes softmax top-k through `topk_gating`. #57246 fixes the eager `torch.topk` fallback in `fused_topk_bias`, and #55147 fixes the width of the `topk_ids` buffer AITER's kernels write; neither changes which AITER launcher is used.

## Claims

- On the same nightly, with only this PR and ROCm/aiter#6306 added, `topk_gating` is **+0.3% to +1.5% output-throughput geomean per cell** (one Qwen3-30B cell reads +5.3% because of a single slow control row; +0.7% without it) at three `max-num-batched-tokens` (2048, 4096, 8192) across two model families: Flash-Next (E=512, top-10, one shared expert) at TP2 with `VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER_FUSION_SHARED_EXPERTS` 1 and 0, and Qwen3-30B-A3B (E=128, top-8) at TP1. Single-stream decode (1k/1k, concurrency 1) is the largest effect: +2.1% to +4.8% in all 9 cells.
- No row with a reproducible loss. 7 of 54 rows are negative; all but one are within −2.2% of control, and the exception (Qwen3-30B 8k/128 c8 at mbt 8192, −8.6%) is a noisy row, discussed below. Mean TTFT is flat within noise (cell geomeans −1.5% to +0.8%).
- Kernel: for E=512 / top-10 with one shared expert, `topk_gating` is 37–47% faster than `topk_softmax(..., "sigmoid")` at T ≤ 1024 and level at T = 2048–4096 (AITER op test, table below).
- No directional accuracy change: GSM8K on all 9 cells, paired, 134 problems fixed and 126 broken in total (net +8 of 11,871), no cell below p = 0.12.
- gfx942 (MI300-class, added in the last commit): the kernel passes AITER's op test and the vLLM unit tests there with zero routing mismatches, is neutral to slightly positive end to end (+2.0% and +0.45% output-throughput geomean on Qwen3-30B at mbt 4096 and 2048; +1.4% to +4.7% on three Flash-Next-FP8 TP2 rows), and GSM8K is unchanged (no cell below p = 0.16). This is a lighter check than the gfx950 sweep; details below.
- Which launches change: on gfx942 and gfx950, contiguous gating rows with T ≤ 4096 tokens, and shared experts only with sigmoid scoring and 1, 2, 4 or 8 shared experts. Everything else, and every other GPU architecture, keeps `topk_softmax`. There is no env var to turn it off.

- **Qwen3-30B-A3B rows with a large swing.** Three rows move by more than 5%, all of them 8k prefill-heavy rows: 8k/1k c32 at mbt 2048 (+31.6%, because the control ran at 679 tok/s against 953–1125 in the other two 8k/1k c32 runs on this model), 8k/128 c8 at mbt 8192 (−8.6%) and 8k/1k c32 at mbt 8192 (+6.4%). With one repeat these rows cannot be separated from noise, but the −8.6% row is not explained either: the same case is −2.2% at mbt 2048 and +0.6% at mbt 4096, and a repeat of 8k/128 c8 on Qwen3-30B is the open item.
- **Gain at small `T`, flat at large `T`.** Concurrency 1 gains +2.1% to +4.8% in every cell, while the 8k and 16k rows sit at about +0.5% to +1%: their steps are dominated by prefill chunks of 2048 tokens or more, where the kernel is level with the legacy launcher (and above 4096 the legacy launcher runs in both arms). - **FSE does not change the picture.** With FSE=1 the candidate also fuses the sigmoid shared-expert scoring (the suffix from #6306); with FSE=0 `topk_gating` runs without it. The cell geomeans are +1.1% to +1.5% and +0.7% to +1.2%.
- **TTFT.** Prefill is where `topk_gating` helps least, so the TTFT geomean is flat: −1.5% to +0.8% across the nine cells, which is the size of the noise in a single repeat.

## Details

**What runs before and after.** Before, every softmax top-k on ROCm with AITER (`FusedTopKRouter`, and the fused-shared-expert router) ran `topk_softmax`, AITER's legacy launcher. After, `dispatch_topk_softmax_func` takes the launch's tensors and shared-expert arguments and returns `rocm_aiter_ops.topk_gating` on gfx942 and gfx950 when `_aiter_topk_gating_supported` accepts the launch, and `topk_softmax` otherwise (including on every other architecture). `_aiter_topk_gating_supported` accepts contiguous gating rows with at most 4096 tokens; with shared experts it also needs sigmoid scoring, 1, 2, 4 or 8 of them, `topk_weights` and `topk_indices` sharing a row stride, and `topk_weights` wide enough for the shared columns. The shared-expert ids stay as the caller prefilled them, as with the legacy launcher. `_rocm_aiter_topk_gating_impl` is a plain call into `aiter.ops.topk.topk_gating`; the router runs inside the opaque `moe_forward` custom op, so nothing here changes graph capture.

**Why the 4096-token cap.** At E=512 / top-10 the kernel is level with the legacy launcher at T = 2048 and 4096 and was slower at 8K-token prefill chunks in an earlier measurement, so above 4096 the legacy launcher keeps serving. The cap is a named constant in the router, not a magic number.

**Why no env var.** The sweep shows a small positive effect at every shape tested on gfx950 and no reproducible loss, so there is nothing to opt into, and the path is the default under the existing AITER enablement (`VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER`, `VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER_MOE`) on gfx942 and gfx950. gfx942 was verified more lightly than gfx950 (see the gfx942 section); the gate is one line (`is_topk_gating_enabled`), so another architecture can be added the same way once it is measured.

**CUDA and other platforms.** No shared code path changes on CUDA: the dispatch change is under the ROCm AITER branch of `dispatch_topk_softmax_func`, and the non-AITER path still returns `vllm_topk_softmax`. On ROCm GPUs other than gfx942 and gfx950 nothing changes.

**AITER dependency.** The shared-expert path needs `num_shared_experts` on `topk_gating` from ROCm/aiter#6306. There is no import fallback and no feature probe in this PR: it is not mergeable against an AITER release without #6306, which is why it is a draft. The stock `nightly-81198e97` AITER lacks the argument, so the candidate image patches in only #6306.

**Limitations and CI gaps.**

- **gfx950 unit tests at the head.** The gfx950 pass counts above are from before the gfx942 commit; the new arch test was run on gfx942 only.
- **One repeat per cell.** Single rows carry about ±2% noise (and more on the Qwen3-30B 8k rows); the claim is the direction and size across 54 rows, not any one row. No repeats were run, to limit node time.
- **TP and EP.** Clean arms exist for TP2 (Flash-Next) and TP1 (Qwen3-30B). TP8 and EP were only run on non-clean images (supplementary section above).
- **GPU generation.** gfx950 has the full sweep. gfx942 (MI300-class) has a lighter check: the kernel op test, the unit tests, Qwen3-30B at mbt 2048 and 4096, and one Flash-Next-FP8 TP2 cell with three perf rows and GSM8K; no FSE=0, no mbt 8192, and the Flash-Next perf comparison spans two cells because of two slow cards on the node. The `_AITER_TOPK_GATING_MAX_TOKENS` cap was not retuned for gfx942. Other architectures keep the legacy launcher.
- **Models.** Two router shapes (E=512/top-10 with a shared expert, E=128/top-8). Qwen3-Next and Qwen3.5 share the first router; larger siblings of Qwen3-30B share the second. Nothing with grouped top-k or a bias is routed here, by construction.
- **Not exercised.** NIAH, MTP/spec-decode, and prompts beyond 16k. The router never sees more than `max-num-batched-tokens` tokens per step, so longer prompts repeat the same chunk sizes, and the router output does not depend on context length except through `T`.
- **Baseline for textual churn.** There is no control-versus-control GSM8K run, so how much of the 82–84% textual difference is run-to-run non-determinism is not measured.
- **CI.** Validation ran outside CI on MI355X (gfx950) and MI300-class (gfx942). AITER's own CI runs the `shared` section of `op_tests/test_moe_topk_gating.py` by default, so the kernel cases are reached there.
