# [ROCm][Muse Spark] Support EPLB with FP8 and BF16 experts

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/63729) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description

## Overview

`--enable-eplb` failed at init for Muse Spark on ROCm (`MuseSpark1x FP8 on ROCm does not support EPLB`). Nothing in the model needs that restriction for the Triton FP8 and BF16 paths:

- the post-expert RMSNorm has one `[H]` weight shared by all experts, so EPLB does not need to move it;
- the Triton experts keep the expert dimension outermost in every weight and scale, so EPLB's row moves are layout-safe (`QuarkW8A8Fp8MoEMethod` just never declared `supports_eplb`);
- `RoutedExperts.load_weights` already fans per-expert checkpoint tensors out to every physical replica.

This PR:

1. passes `num_redundant_experts` / `enable_eplb` from the EPLB config into the MoE block;
2. declares `supports_eplb` for the Muse Quark FP8 Triton method;
3. loads consolidated (all-experts-stacked) tensors into every initial physical replica;
4. gives `MuseSpark1xRouter` an `EplbLayerState`. `FusedMoEFactory` only creates and attaches EPLB state to routers it builds itself, and Muse passes its own router. Without this, the router neither mapped logical to physical expert ids nor recorded expert load, while EPLB still moved weights.

MXFP4 (AITER expanded-input experts) keeps rejecting EPLB; it is untested.

## Claims

- EPLB loads and runs for Muse Spark PTPC-FP8 at TP8 + EP on 8× MI300X and keeps GSM8K accuracy (95.45% vs 95.07% flexible-extract without EPLB, within ±0.6%).
- Serving with `--eplb-config '{"num_redundant_experts":32,"communicator":"pynccl","use_async":false}'` against EP without EPLB: +2.2–3.1% total throughput at 8k input and +4.0–6.5% at 16k input for concurrency 32–64 (1k output), within ±1.8% at 8k and 16k for concurrency 1–16, and median TTFT 11–27% lower at every 8k and 16k point. At 1k input it is 1.7–5.2% slower.
- On ROCm the EPLB communicator should be set explicitly. Left unset, it resolves to NIXL; on MI300X its async rearrangements did not complete before the next one started, and serving was 9–68% slower than without EPLB. `pynccl` and `torch_nccl` require `"use_async": false`.

## Validation

**Unit tests.** `test_consolidated_loader_fills_every_initial_redundant_replica` (new, in `tests/models/test_muse_spark_rocm_loading.py`) loads consolidated expert tensors with 4 logical and 6 physical experts and checks that every physical expert `p` receives logical expert `p % 4`, EPLB's initial placement; it fails without this PR (only the first 4 physical experts are loaded). With the existing Muse Spark loading, logits and BF16 MoE tests: 44 passed, 3 skipped on MI300X (`vllm/vllm-openai-rocm:nightly-5d35ce3934` with this branch's files mounted).

**Serving.** `amd/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B-Quark-PTPC-FP8`, TP8 + `--enable-expert-parallel --moe-backend triton`, 8× MI300X, `vllm/vllm-openai-rocm:nightly-83c078d955cd6e0c63d3b12abe669c8de936a370` with this branch's files mounted. `VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER=1`, `--attention-backend ROCM_AITER_FA`, `FULL_AND_PIECEWISE` CUDA graphs, `--max-num-batched-tokens 8192 --max-num-seqs 128 --max-model-len 32768 --no-enable-prefix-caching`. `vllm bench serve` random dataset, `--random-range-ratio 0 --ignore-eos`, 3× concurrency prompts (4 at c1). Both arms ran on the same node. EPLB config `{"num_redundant_experts":32,"communicator":"pynccl","use_async":false}` with the default window (1000) and step interval (3000); 21 rearrangements completed during the sweep, each in about 0.02 s.

The same sweep with the communicator left unset (NIXL) was 9–68% slower than without EPLB, getting worse as the sweep went on: async rearrangements started every 2–4 minutes and none completed.

**Accuracy.** GSM8K 5-shot (1,319 questions, `local-completions`, 64 concurrent, seed 4321). Expert placement does not depend on the communicator.

| EPLB | 0.9545 ± 0.0057 | 0.9538 ± 0.0058 |

**gfx950.** This was just a light validation with `--load-format dummy`: PTPC-FP8, TP4 + EP + `--moe-backend triton` on 4× MI355X, EPLB with 16 redundant experts, `pynccl`, `use_async: false` and a 64-step interval. It served all requests and completed 244 rearrangements. At 4 ranks the redundant experts take most of the KV cache (863k → 203k tokens), so 8k/1k c64 queued; the other points were 1.3–4.2% slower than without EPLB, as expected with dummy-weight routing. MXFP4 keeps rejecting EPLB (`validate_mxfp4_deployment`), unchanged.
