# [Bugfix][ROCm] Use 64-bit row offsets in the Triton W4A16 GEMM

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/63054) · state: closed · merged: True

## Original PR description

## Purpose

Fixes #63049. `triton_w4a16_gemm_kernel` indexes activations and outputs with int32 row offsets, which wrap once M × K or M × N passes 2^31 and fault on ROCm. Casting `pid_m` to int64 makes both products 64-bit. The weight, scale and zero-point offsets do not depend on M and stay int32.

## Test Plan

The new test runs the GEMM one block of rows past each bound, (K, N) = (256, 34816) for the output offsets and (17408, 256) for the activation offsets, and compares the last 64 rows with the float32 reference already in the file.

I also called `_triton_w4a16_gemm_impl` alone at a Qwen3.5 27B model's shapes, served `vllm-sr/d3-W4A16-AWQ` and `vllm-sr/d3-W4A16-GPTQ` at the default 262,144-token length on one MI300X, and timed the kernel before and after the change.

## Test Result

The new test passes, along with the rest of the file. Before the change the kernel faults at M = 62,000 with (K, N) = (5120, 34816) and at M = 124,000 with (17408, 5120). After it, both shapes run up to M = 262,144, and their last rows are as close to a float32 matmul as at small M. Both packages now serve at the default length, and their answers on 300 requests match those of runs at `--max-model-len 32768`, which did not reach the bound. Over three projection shapes, M from 1 to 32,768, with and without zero points, every timing is within 2.6% of the unchanged kernel, in both directions.
