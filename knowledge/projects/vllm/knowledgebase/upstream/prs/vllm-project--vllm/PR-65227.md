# [Perf][Multimodal] Encoder CUDA graphs: one replay per batch, capture only budgets that beat eager

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/65227) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description



## Overview

Encoder CUDA graphs only save eager's host launch time. On main they are also captured and replayed where eager is GPU-bound, and a batch can be split into several padded replays. On those batches the graph is slower than eager: up to 2.7x on MiniMax-M3 and 1.7x on DeepSeek-V4.1. This PR replays each batch at most once, and captures only the budgets whose replay beats eager. Everything larger runs as one eager forward. The capture budgets are now chosen by measurement rather than by `max_num_batched_tokens`.

Stacked on #65084 (finer budget grid, shared capture buffers) and #65226 (MiniMax zero-length padding; #65084 needs it to stay correct on MiniMax). Only the last commit belongs to this PR.

**Status: draft.** Across six measured encoder models, two batches are still slower than main, and a fix is being measured:
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 4 x 448x896 (2048 tokens): 30.8 → 32.7 ms. The 20% margin drops the 2048 budget (probe replay/eager 0.92), but light batches that fill it still gain about 6%.
- InternVL3-8B, 3 images (2304 tokens): 11.9 → 14.4 ms. Above 1024 the grid steps 1.5x; the next budget after 2048 (3072) is dropped, so the batch runs eagerly. Main splits it into two replays.

The candidate fix is quarter-octave budgets above 1024 and a smaller margin.

## Claims

- **No batch slower than eager.** Over 28–34 encoder batches per model (small, medium, large, wide, unaligned, mixed), the graph path is at most 1.02x packed eager on all six measured models. On main it reaches 2.7x (MiniMax) and 1.4–1.8x on the others.
- **Faster.** Geomean encoder latency vs main is 1.11x–1.30x (table below). #65084 alone gives 1.16x on DeepSeek; on MiniMax it is incorrect without #65226.
- **Less memory.** Graphs above the measured limit are never captured. Capture memory drops 6–10x, for example from 5.12 to 0.81 GiB (MiniMax) and from 6.46 to 0.63 GiB (DeepSeek).
- **GLM-4.1V fix.** GLM-4.1V reserved `token_budget // max_batch_size` tokens per capture slot. With #65084's budgets that are not multiples of 64, a 336x336 image (144 tokens) in the 160 budget overflowed the buffer and crashed. It now rounds up, as Qwen2.5-VL already does (#40830).
- **Small batches unchanged.** They keep their graphs: 2–12 ms vs 17–21 ms eager on MiniMax, 7–23 ms vs ~30 ms eager on DeepSeek.

## Details

**Why a graph can lose.** Eager latency is max(host launch time, GPU time); a replay costs the GPU time of its captured shape. Once eager is GPU-bound there is no launch time left to save, and the padding up to the next budget is pure loss. On main this happens at every budget above about 2K tokens (MiniMax) or 1K (DeepSeek). The default budgets go up to `max_num_batched_tokens`.

**One replay per batch.** The whole batch replays the smallest budget that holds it, or runs as one eager forward. Greedy packing (#58925) split batches to avoid power-of-two cliffs. With #65084's finer grid and no graphs past the limit, splitting only adds replays and padding.

**Capture only what beats eager.** `capture()` first probes budgets from the smallest up, in a throwaway pool. Each dummy batch runs once eagerly and once as a replay. The first budget whose replay is not at least 20% faster than its eager forward stops the probe; it and all larger budgets are never captured, and their batches run eagerly.
- The rule needs no per-model or per-GPU threshold.
- The 20% margin covers real batches whose attention costs more than the dummy's many short sequences. Without it (bare replay < eager), MiniMax kept 3072, and four batches near it ran 3–25% slower than eager.
- User-specified `encoder_cudagraph_token_budgets` are always captured as given.

**DeepSeek eager fallback.** `encoder_eager_forward` ran one forward per image (~31 ms each, 2.0 s for 64 images). It now runs one unpadded varlen batch: the same forward the graph captures, so outputs are bitwise equal to the graph path. Batches already selected whole are no longer re-concatenated.

**Limitations and trade-offs.**
- Probing replays graphs inside `graph_capture`, before custom all-reduce registers their buffers. It therefore runs only when the encoder has no collectives: TP=1 or `mm_encoder_tp_mode="data"`, which the MiniMax-M3, DeepSeek-V4.1, Kimi-K2.5 and Qwen3-VL recipes use. TP-sharded encoders keep the full budget set.
- The probe adds 2–4 s to capture (MiniMax 6.8 → 9.2 s, DeepSeek 4.2 → 8.0 s).
- Timing near the cut can differ between ranks or restarts; data-mode ranks dispatch locally, so that is safe.
- A batch with more images than `max_batch_size` runs eagerly instead of being split. Below the limit this needs more than 64 images in under ~2K tokens.
- Evaluated on MiniMax-M3.1-Flash and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on GB200. Other encoder-graph models get the same dispatch; their budgets are probed the same way.
- Follow-up, not in this PR: DeepSeek reserves 9 patch rows per output token, but real images use 7.7–8.7, so graph replays carry another 3–17% unused rows (0.5–2 ms per batch). Fixing it needs a row capacity in dispatch.

**Not a duplicate.** #65084 refines the grid and shares buffers but keeps greedy multi-replay dispatch and captures up to `max_num_batched_tokens`; this PR builds on it. #60112 falls back to eager when a replay does not fit its buffers; it is a safety net and does not change dispatch, capture range or GLM's capacity.

**Tests.** `pytest tests/v1/cudagraph/test_encoder_cudagraph.py`: 78 passed (GB200). On the earlier base, the encoder runner and GPU profiler tests also passed (174). `pre-commit` passes.
