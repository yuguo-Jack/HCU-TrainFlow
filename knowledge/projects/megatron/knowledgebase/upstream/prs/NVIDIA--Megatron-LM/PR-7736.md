# What does this PR do?

Creates the dynamic DPxCP groups and the expert data parallel gloo group with the configured `distributed_timeout_minutes`, like every other group in `initialize_model_parallel()`.

`create_dynamic_dp_cp_groups()` calls `create_group()` without a `timeout`, so with `dynamic_context_parallel=True` each dynamic DPxCP group falls back to torch's default NCCL timeout of 10 minutes, and the NCCL watchdog aborts on those groups after 10 minutes whatever `distributed_timeout_minutes` says. The expert data parallel gloo group has the same omission and always gets gloo's default of 30 minutes. We ran into this in a job with dynamic context parallelism and a 30-minute distributed timeout: after one rank stalled, the watchdog fired on the dynamic DPxCP groups at 10 minutes.

- `create_dynamic_dp_cp_groups()` takes an optional `timeout` and passes it to `create_group()`; `initialize_model_parallel()` passes the configured timeout.
- The expert data parallel gloo group is created with the same timeout.
- `test_dynamic_dp_cp_groups` initializes with `distributed_timeout_minutes=45` and checks every dynamic group's timeout; the new `test_expert_data_parallel_gloo_group_timeout` checks the gloo group.

Behaviour changes only with dynamic context parallelism enabled, or, for the expert gloo group, when `distributed_timeout_minutes` is not 30.

- sha7doww · comment · 2026-10-13T05:08:42Z · [source](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Megatron-LM/pull/7736#issuecomment-6138696415)

Thanks for the review. Group creation wasn't the slow part. The groups are created at startup, and the `timeout` passed at creation applies to every operation on the group after that. Our job hung in the first training step: three ranks of a `DYNAMIC_DP_CP_GROUP_4` group were stuck on a ring-attention P2P recv while recomputing activations in the backward pass, and the ring was waiting on the fourth rank. The watchdog fired on that group after 10 minutes.

That fourth rank had most likely already exited the step with an error. Our training framework, which runs on top of Megatron, didn't report it, so we don't know what the error was. When we replayed the same config, the ranks that dropped out had run out of GPU memory. We didn't find anything pointing to an inefficiency in Megatron.
