# GTP: bind eager-chain weight buffers at issue time and pack them into one slab

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Megatron-LM/pull/8545) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description


- [ ] I, the PR author, have personally reviewed every line of this PR.

## Problem

`GTPWeightCache` pins one buffer per cache key (shape, dtype, direction, chain) for the whole
run. On an Ultra rank at GTP 64 that is 217 buffers, 17 GB, although only the current layer, the
prefetched next layer and a little scratch are ever in flight: about 1 GB during the backward and
1.2 GB between steps. The forward buffers sit idle through the whole backward and optimizer step,
the backward buffers through the whole forward.

## What this PR does

Eager (non-CUDA-graph) chains stop pinning their buffers. CUDA-graph chains keep the pooled
buffers with fixed addresses, since replay needs them. No new command-line option: the new
policy is the default, it falls back by itself where the assumptions do not hold, and
`GTP_CONFIG.eager_buffer_policy` (`"slab"` | `"transient"` | `"pooled"`) remains an internal
knob for tests and diagnosis.

**Transient buffers (the warm-up and the fallback).** `acquire(ticket, producer_stream)`
allocates the buffer when the collective is issued and the buffer is freed once its consumer
has been enqueued. Because the consumer is enqueued only after the call that returned the
buffer (and the next layer's prefetch is issued from inside that call), `consume` tags the
buffer with a *consume epoch* (`begin_consume` bumps it at the top of every call that hands
gathered weights to a consumer) and the buffer is freed at the first `acquire` of a later
epoch. Reduce-scatter outputs are freed by `release` right after `main_grad.add_`. The wgrad
GEMM output and FP32-RS all-to-all scratch follow the same rule and record the reduce-scatter
stream before they are dropped. The writing collective waits on an event of the allocating
stream; a reader on another stream is recorded on the buffer.

**The weight slab (steady state).** A training step's schedule is static, so step 2 runs
transient while every acquire and free is recorded (step 1 is skipped: its prefetch links are
still forming); at its boundary the recorded lifetimes are packed largest-first into one slab
(each region at the lowest offset clear of every region live at the same time; a time-bucket
index keeps the plan at about a second for 22k regions) and from step 3 each (ticket, n-th
acquire of the step) reuses its fixed slice, MXFP8 tensors built once over the slice. The
steady-state path is a position lookup: the k-th acquire of a step binds the k-th recorded
region, and the plan holds for every region the regions that last used each of its bytes, so
the writer waits only on those (the previous reader when it ran on another stream, the previous
writer when nobody read it). No per-step allocation, no `record_stream`, no per-acquire search;
the one-GPU micro-benchmark puts the whole acquire/consume/free round trip at about 6 µs against
1.5 µs for the pooled lookup, which is 0.1 s per step at 22k acquires.

**Keeping the run's peak in the steady state.** Three details decide whether the recording
phase, not the steady state, sets the run's peak. Consumed buffers are freed at the step
boundary, where every consumer is enqueued, instead of at the next step's first acquire. The
cache is released once before the recording step and once before the slab is allocated, so the
optimizer state, the recording and the slab start from a compact pool. And the largest buffers
(the vocabulary tables, 5 GB each) get fixed slices only if the optimizer step is not the run's
peak, read from the rise of the maximum allocated through the optimizer step before the
recording: at a forward/backward peak the table gathers are live, so their slices cost nothing
and end the per-step churn of the largest buffers; when the optimizer step is the peak (Muon at
small data parallelism) no gather is live there, and the tables stay transient.

**Where the assumptions do not hold.** The first acquire that leaves the recorded order (a
skipped prefetch, a replayed layer) ends the slab for that step: the rest of the step runs on
transient buffers and the step boundary fences every stream that touched the slab; the counts
appear in the memory report (`gtp buffers: slab ... conflicts N, misses M`). Two such steps in
a row mean the schedule changed (a batch-size ramp, for example): the plan and the slab are
dropped and re-recorded from the next two steps; after three failed plans the run stays
transient. Evaluation passes are bracketed by `set_forward_only` and run on transient buffers
without touching the plan. `finalize_model_grads` calls `step_boundary()` once per step.

## Results (same running config in every phase, same ranks, main's unconditional code otherwise)

This PR alone on top of main, same running config, same ranks.

64 GPUs, production shapes (GTP 64 / EP 8 / EGTP 8, 164 layers, 4 micro-batches, Muon, mock data
with real routing, 16 steps, job 1400953). Main is a pristine checkout of 3e66acdb6 built in its own
worktree. Steady = max over ranks and steps 6 to 15; the run's peak is the steady state (step 1
207 GiB, the recording step 230 GiB).

| phase | step time | steady allocated | steady reserved | device memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| main | 33.6 s | 213.1 GiB | 254.6 GiB | 275.7 GiB |
| PR (default) | 33.65 s (+0.1%) | 197.3 GiB (-15.8) | 238.5 GiB (-16.1) | 259.6 GiB (-16.1) |

Reserved at this scale varies by about +-7 GiB between runs of identical code (two runs of the same
PR tree measured 227.8 and 241.6 GiB on identical allocated); allocated is the stable signal. Plan:
21,972 regions in a 4.7 GB slab, zero conflicts or misses, the three vocabulary-table tickets
transient; one acquire costs ~6 us of host time (pooled lookup 1.4 us).

16 GPUs, reduced layout (GTP 16 / EP 2 / EGTP 8, mock data with real routing, Adam, 1 micro-batch,
16 steps, job 1400952):

| phase | step time | steady allocated | steady reserved | device memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| main (pooled) | 1.26 s | 89.8 GiB | 104.7 GiB | 118.5 GiB |
| PR (default) | 1.26 s | 82.9 GiB (-6.9) | 101.3 GiB (-3.5) | 115.0 GiB (-3.5) |

With `weight_slab_max_bytes=None` (the tables pinned) the 16-GPU steady reserved was 98.3 GiB in
three runs; at 64 GPUs the same setting cost 7 GiB of allocated, so the default keeps the tables
transient. Stacked with the offload-stream PR the savings add (16 GPUs -5.3 GiB, 64 GPUs -18 GiB).

256 GPUs, EP 64 house layout (GTP 64 / EP 64 / EGTP 4, 82 layers, 1 micro-batch, global batch 256,
Adam, mock data with forced balancing, nsys on rank 0, 16 steps, job 1360166), main and PR twice each,
interleaved on the same ranks; numbers are the mean of the two phases, steady = steps 6 to 15:
| phase | step time (mean / median) | steady allocated | steady reserved | resident | device memory at peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| main | 3.224 / 3.226 s | 166.9 GiB | 185.7 GiB | 128.8 GiB | 206.9 GiB |
| PR, default (slab) | 3.240 / 3.233 s (+0.5% / +0.2%) | 160.3 GiB (-6.7, -4.0%) | 180.3 GiB (-5.4, -2.9%) | 112.1 GiB (-16.8) | 201.4 GiB (-5.6, -2.7%) |

(These 256-GPU numbers are from an earlier tree of this PR with two recording steps; the final
tree's 256-GPU run is pending and will replace them.) The two main phases differ by 0.9% in step
time, so the PR's +0.5% is inside the spread. The plan is
2,699 regions in a 4.5 GB slab (3.9 GB live at the peak), zero conflicts or misses. The reserved
saving is smaller than at 64 GPUs: with one micro-batch per step the pinned cache is smaller to begin
with, and the two 5 GB vocabulary-table gathers (above `weight_slab_max_bytes`, transient) and the
activations reuse the memory the slab frees. Resident memory, the part held between steps, drops
16.8 GiB at both scales.
