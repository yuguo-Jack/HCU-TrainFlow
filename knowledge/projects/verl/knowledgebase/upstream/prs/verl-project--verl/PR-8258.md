# [hardware, perf, doc] feat: speed up the Qwen3-4B TPU GRPO example by 14% with three overlap settings

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/verl-project/verl/pull/8258) · state: closed · merged: False

## Original PR description

### What does this PR do?

> This PR tunes the rollout/training overlap of the Qwen3-4B TPU GRPO example ([`examples/tpu/grpo/run_qwen3_4b_torchtitan.sh`](https://github.com/jialei777/verl-upstream/blob/perf/tpu-grpo-4b-overlap-config/examples/tpu/grpo/run_qwen3_4b_torchtitan.sh)). It changes the defaults of three settings, which are now environment variables:
>
> | setting | env var | before | after |
> |---|---|---:|---:|
> | `trainer.v1.separate_async.num_warmup_batches` | `NUM_WARMUP_BATCHES` | 1 | **2** |
> | `actor_rollout_ref.rollout.max_num_batched_tokens` | `MAX_NUM_BATCHED_TOKENS` | 1536 (`MAX_MODEL_LEN`) | **4096** |
> | `actor_rollout_ref.actor.torchtitan.pad_to_length_bucket` | `PAD_TO_LENGTH_BUCKET` | 1024 | **512** |
>
> Over 100 steps on 2× v6e-4 (4 trainer + 4 rollout chips), the mean step time drops from **25.2–25.6 s to 21.6 s (−14–16%)**. The 100 steps take **37.7 min instead of 43.3–44.0 min**, and the final GSM8K validation accuracy is the same (0.953 vs 0.938–0.945). It is a config-only change.
>
> The bottlenecks were found with an RL timeline, built with the timeline tooling from the verl-hardware-plugin branch [`feat/tpu-rl-timeline`](https://github.com/jialei777/verl-hardware-plugin/tree/feat/tpu-rl-timeline) (jialei777/verl-hardware-plugin#9). It draws, per step, what the trainer chips and the rollout chips are doing (figures below).
>
> **Trade-off:** the second queued batch makes training samples staler (about 0.4 weight versions), so the run learns less per step early on. Measured against time instead of steps, reward progress roughly breaks even with the baseline. `NUM_WARMUP_BATCHES=1.5` (or `1`) trades some of the speedup for fresher samples. Details are under *Test*.

Follows up on #8226 (which introduced the 1024-token buckets with micro batch 4) and #8239 (micro batch 2).

#### What each setting contributes (20-step runs, mean over steps 10–20, no validation)

| run | `timing_s/step` | trainer waits | rollout tok/s | step s per 1k generated tokens |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| baseline | 30.0 s | 7.85 s | 5.74k | 0.175 |
| 2 batches ahead | 28.9 s | 4.8 s | 6.36k | 0.156 |
| 2 batches ahead + 4096 batched tokens | 27.8 s | 3.8 s | 6.44k | 0.154 |

The 512-token buckets only matter once the trainer is the bottleneck, from about step 60. Over 100 steps, they bring the steps 81–100 from 20.2 s to 18.4 s, and `update_actor` costs 3–8% less per trained token.

#### Learning per step vs. per minute

All runs below except the baselines use 4096 batched tokens and 512-token buckets. The reward columns use the 10-step moving average of `critic/score/mean`. "0.90 reached" counts minutes of cumulative step time; the area column averages the reward between 10 and 35 min of step time (higher is better).

| run | `NUM_WARMUP_BATCHES` | mean step | 100 steps | staleness | reward, steps 11–50 / 51–100 | 0.90 reached | reward area, 10–35 min | val acc at step 100 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| baseline (2 runs) | 1 (1536 tok, 1024 buckets) | 25.2–25.6 s | 43.3–44.0 min | 0.90 | 0.853–0.858 / 0.943–0.947 | 21.3–22.1 min | 0.886–0.892 | 0.938–0.945 |
| one batch ahead | 1 | 26.7 s | 45.8 min | 0.83 | 0.860 / 0.943 | 20.4 min | 0.895 | 0.930 |
| 1.5 batches ahead (48 prompts) | 1.5 | 22.4 s | 38.7 min | 1.20 | 0.824 / 0.939 | 20.4 min | 0.882 | 0.945 |
| **this PR** | **2** | **21.6 s** | **37.7 min** | 1.32 | 0.812 / 0.929 | 23.4 min | 0.872 | **0.953** |

- **More batches ahead means faster steps but staler samples and less learning per step.** In reward over time the two roughly cancel; the spread in area is small.
- **With a fixed 100-step budget,** every configuration ends at the same validation accuracy (±0.02 with 128 samples), and 2 or 1.5 batches ahead get there 5–7 min sooner.
- **Fractional values work:** in separate-async mode `data.gen_batch_size` is forced to 1, so `NUM_WARMUP_BATCHES=1.5` queues 48 prompts.

### Design & Code Changes

| setting | why |
|---|---|
| `NUM_WARMUP_BATCHES=2` | With one batch ahead, a batch is queued only one step before it is consumed, but its 1024-token responses need about 35 s at about 30 tok/s per request. The rollout then idles at batch ends while the trainer waits, steps swing between ~19 s and 27–37 s, and the decode slots average 63–75% full. A second queued batch refills freed slots at once (84–94% full, no rollout idle time). |
| `MAX_NUM_BATCHED_TOKENS=4096` | Every weight sync aborts the in-flight rollouts (about 230 with two batches ahead). They resume as partial rollouts and must re-prefill their prompt plus partial response, about 130k tokens per sync. A larger step budget gets them decoding again within at most 1.9 s instead of 3.0 s, with no change to decode speed. 8192 adds nothing more. |
| `PAD_TO_LENGTH_BUCKET=512` | With `MICRO_BATCH_SIZE=2`, a packed actor micro-batch late in training is about 1,090 tokens, which 1024-token buckets pad to 2,048. 512-token buckets waste less, at the cost of a few extra compiled shapes (about +1.9 s per step in steps 1–10). |

`README.md`: the expected 4B step time changes from "20-30 s (~25 s average)" to "18-27 s (~22 s average over 100 steps)".

**Tried, not included:**
- **512 decode slots:** each request decodes slower, and throughput tops out below 256 full slots.
- **`gpu_memory_utilization=0.85`:** 256 running requests use at most about 40% of the KV cache at 0.6, so it changes nothing.
- **16 `tpu-sync` transfer streams:** the transfer is limited by network bandwidth (~1.1 s either way).
- **`parameter_sync_step=2`:** +6% rollout throughput, but it changes the algorithm.

**Other trade-offs:**
- **Longer validation** (5.4 vs 3.9–4.1 min per 100 steps): validation requests queue behind the extra training batch.
- **Weight-sync pause spikes:** in about a quarter of the syncs, the rollout pause takes 1–7 s instead of 0.1–0.2 s. `vLLMHttpServer.abort_all_requests` waits until every request already handed to vLLM has produced its first token, and some requests are queued behind 256 full slots. Releasing that barrier once a request reaches the engine (as in jialei777/verl-upstream#59) would remove these spikes.

**Noticed along the way, not addressed here** (same in every run, including the baseline):
- **The learning rate never decays.** `TorchTitanEngine.__init__` (`verl/workers/engine/torchtitan/transformer_impl.py`) computes `total_training_steps` but does not pass it to TorchTitan's `TrainingConfig(steps=...)`, so cosine decay runs over TorchTitan's default of 10,000 steps (`actor/lr` is still 1.9996e-6 at step 100).
- **Trainer waits for samples come in 2 s steps.** `ReplayBuffer` waits with `time.sleep(poll_interval=2.0)`, so each waiting step loses about 1 s on average.
- **`actor/grad_norm` spikes to the same values in every run** (about 26.4k, 1,051 and 90). They are clipped by `max_norm=1.0`.
