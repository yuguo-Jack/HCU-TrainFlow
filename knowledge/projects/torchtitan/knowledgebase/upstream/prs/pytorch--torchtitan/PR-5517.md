# [moe][perf] Shared experts on a side stream in CUDA graphs at inference (2/3)

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/pytorch/torchtitan/pull/5517) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description

**TLDR:** At inference under CUDA graphs, `MoE.forward` runs the shared experts on a side stream, overlapped with the routed experts. At EP = 4 with Dist-MoE, one MoE block gets 6-7% faster on Qwen3.5 and 1.1-1.3% faster on Kimi K2.5 at decode sizes. Outputs bitwise identical.

**Stack** (3 PRs, merge bottom-up; this PR is in **bold**)

| # | PR | Independent? | Needs (stack positions) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dist-MoE: skip the per-expert token count it doesn't read | yes | — |
| **2** | **Shared experts on a side stream in CUDA graphs at inference** | **yes** | **—** |
| 3 | Compile the MoE router and its load-balance sums | yes | — |

**Problem**
1. `MoE.forward` runs the router, then the routed experts, then the shared experts, all on one stream.
2. At decode, Dist-MoE's routed path starts with a chain of small kernels (publish, peer barrier, routing, plan; ~14 us) that leaves most SMs idle.
3. The shared experts (two GEMMs, SwiGLU, sigmoid gate; ~17 us for Qwen3.5 at T = 64) read only `x_TD`, yet wait for the whole routed path.
4. Cost: ~11 us per Qwen3.5 MoE block at decode that could overlap.

**Solution**
1. `_shared_experts_side_stream()` returns one side stream per device, or `None` with grad on, under torch.compile, outside CUDA graph capture, with sequence parallelism (SP), or for CPU inputs.
2. With a side stream, `MoE.forward` forks right after the router: the side stream runs `self.shared_experts(x_TD)`, and the main stream waits on it before the shared add.
3. **No collective on the side stream.** Dist-MoE's dispatch/combine and the MoE's one TP all-reduce stay on the main stream, after the join (without SP the shared input is replicated and its output stays TP-partial).
4. **SP keeps them inline:** the shared experts all-gather and reduce-scatter themselves.
5. Unchanged: training, eager forwards, the subclasses that override `forward` (Muse Spark, Kimi K3 latent MoE), and the math: the same kernels run on the same inputs.

You may ask:
- Why fork after the router? Forking before it is slower in every row (Qwen3.5 T = 64: 149.2 us): the shared GEMMs then compete with the router's small kernels.
- Why no token cap? vLLM caps its overlap at 256 tokens per rank, but Qwen3.5 still gains up to T = 8192.

NOTE:
- Timed only with Dist-MoE. The stock dispatch paths take the same side stream but weren't timed.
- Kimi K2.5 above T = 256 is not measured; a large shared expert could lose at prefill sizes.
- Graph-pool memory grows a little: the side stream's intermediates can't reuse main-stream blocks (a 24-layer T = 4096 probe: +52 MiB; not measured on the real models).
- The side stream comes from torch's stream pool, so a later `torch.cuda.Stream()` elsewhere can return the same stream. That costs overlap, never correctness.
- The EP = 4 table was timed before the CPU-input guard and the move of the gate from TP to SP; EP = 4 without TP takes the same path under both gates.
- Not run: a full vLLM engine, the H100 TP4 x EP4 generator, and DeepEP / HybridEP capture (which also take the side stream without SP).

**Test plan**
- `pytest tests/unit_tests/gpu/models/common/test_moe_shared_experts_stream.py` (1 and 2 GPUs): graph replay equals eager bitwise; at TP = 2 the side stream is taken without SP and skipped with it, and no collective runs on it (fails if the shared `w2` all-reduces, or with a TP-based gate).
- `pytest tests/unit_tests/cpu/models/common/test_moe.py -k cpu_no_grad`: a CPU no-grad forward never queries CUDA.
