## Agent details

**TLDR:** `gdn_split_in_proj` now compiles twice under the same region name, with Inductor options picked by whether a backward runs. Training: the backward writes each gradient into its slice instead of one masked kernel, GDN layer fwd+bwd -3.7% (Qwen3.5-27B TP2, 16k and 32k tokens). Inference: the four output copies become one kernel, generator GDN layer -3.5 to -4.3 us at decode. Bitwise.

```text
                 stack today (one compile)              this PR
training fwd     4 kernels, runs twice (remat)          2 kernels, runs twice (remat)
training bwd     1 masked kernel: 6-input cat           1 kernel, one copy per gradient slice
inference        4 kernels: q|k|v cat, a, b, gate       1 combo kernel
```

**Problem**
1. **The split.** `_split_in_proj` (5/7) copies `in_proj`'s output, grouped by key head, into `mixed_qkv_TC`, `a_TH`, `b_TH` and `gate_THV`, inside the `gdn_split_in_proj` region.
2. **Backward.** Autograd cats six gradients (q, k, v, z, a, b) back into the grouped layout. Inductor lowers that cat as one kernel that picks a source per element: 237 us at 27B TP2 16k.
3. **Remat.** The split is bare ops outside any remat region, so its forward runs again in backward.
4. **Forward cat.** Its masked q|k|v cat is slow at large T too.
5. **Generator.** At decode the four copies are four launches of ~1.5-2 us each.
6. **Cost.** 571 us of a 6190 us 27B TP2 GDN layer at 16k (9.2%); 7.1 us of a 47.1 us 27B TP4 decode-64 layer.

**Solution**
1. **Two compiles, one region name.** `_split_in_proj_with_grad` and `_split_in_proj_no_grad` wrap the unchanged body, renamed `_split_grouped_in_proj` (a mechanical rename). Two functions, so each gets its own graph cache and recompile limit.
2. **Training options.** `max_pointwise_cat_inputs=0` and `max_complex_pointwise_cat_inputs=0` make Inductor copy each piece into its slice (the knobs `fused_binary_activation` already sets for its backward stack); `combo_kernels=True` launches the remaining copies together.
3. **Inference options.** `combo_kernels=True` only: per-input cat copies would add launches, which dominate at decode sizes.
4. **The pick.** `_split_in_proj` (still the `local_map` boundary) calls the training compile when `projected_TC.requires_grad`. Remat's recompute runs with grad, so it reuses the training compile.
5. **Unchanged:** the region name, the Qwen3.5 configs that enable it, outputs and gradients (the split only moves data).

You may ask:
- Why not one option dict for both? It keeps the whole trainer win in a 3-line diff. But the cat limits make the inference split 2 kernels instead of 1: 1.5-2 us/layer slower at decode than `combo_kernels` alone (27B decode 64 split kernels: 3.84 vs 1.99 us, 1 process), ~0.09 ms (1.1%) of the 7.76 ms decode-64 step; at 35B prefill the two tie. The second compile buys that 1.1%.
- Why not `combo_kernels` alone? Training is unchanged with it (27B 16k 6164 vs 6166 us, 1 process): the backward cat stays one masked kernel.
- Why not compile one function twice? It works, but both option sets would share one 8-graph limit and the dynamic-shape state, with a recompile (`BACKEND_MATCH`) on each set's first call. Two thin wrappers keep them apart, as the `rope` region already does (`rope.py:197`, `:390`).
- Why not cat limits at 3 (the forward's 3-input cat stays masked, the backward's 6-input cat becomes copies)? The masked forward cat is the slow one at large T; it would give up ~130 of the 227 us at 27B 16k (estimate from the per-kernel split, not run).

NOTE: of the GPU tests below, only the new test and the regions-on engine run compile the split (the others clear the regions). Not run: an RL step end to end, the trainer at 64k tokens, TP > 1 timing (per-GPU shapes at TP1), CP. Cold compile time is not measured at model scale; the test compiles both copies in 7.0 s cold vs 4.6 s warm. At prefill 1 x 2048 the layer delta is about twice the split kernels' delta; the rest is unattributed. TP2 smokes (3 steps, regions on, and with spmd typechecking) exit 0. Who reaches it: every Qwen3.5 / 3.6 config that keeps the model's regions (alphabet_sort 9B varlen and 27B perf, terminal-bench 9B and 35B-A3B); batch-invariant recipes clear the regions.

**What this PR does.** The `gdn_split_in_proj` region was one `local_compile` with default options. It becomes two thin wrappers under the same region name, and `_split_in_proj` (still the `local_map` boundary) chooses between them with `projected_TC.requires_grad`. The training copy turns off Inductor's pointwise-cat fusion and turns on `combo_kernels`, so the backward's six-gradient cat becomes per-slice copies instead of one masked kernel. The no-grad copy turns on `combo_kernels` only, so the generator's four outputs come from one launch. The split body is renamed to `_split_grouped_in_proj` without other changes.

**Example.**
```text
trainer, in_proj requires grad:  projected_TC.requires_grad=True  -> _split_in_proj_with_grad

remat recompute (runs with grad) -> same with_grad compile, no extra graphs
generator / no_grad / frozen in_proj: requires_grad=False -> _split_in_proj_no_grad
  before: 4 kernels (q|k|v cat, a, b, gate)    after: 1 combo kernel
```
I picked `requires_grad` as the predicate over `torch.is_grad_enabled()` on purpose. With grad enabled but a frozen `in_proj` and an input that doesn't require grad, no backward runs through the split, so the no-grad options are the right choice there.
