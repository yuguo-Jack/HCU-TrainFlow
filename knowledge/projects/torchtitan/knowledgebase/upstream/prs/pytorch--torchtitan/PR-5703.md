## Why
#5468 places compute chunks by Newton-Schulz cost alone. If a bucket mixes unevenly sharded compute-ready work with redistributed parameters, the ranks with less fixed work can absorb every chunk. Their compute exchange buffer and all-to-all receive volume then grow to the whole bucket, while the other ranks receive nothing. In-tree Kimi recipes keep routed experts in their own buckets, so they don't hit this.

**Example.** 48 `dp_shard` ranks, EP=1, and one bucket holding Kimi K3 layers 3-4 including the routed experts. Storing 896 experts on 48 ranks puts 19 on every rank except rank 47, which gets 3 (fake process group, planning only):

| | largest compute exchange buffer | ranks computing redistributed chunks | largest Newton-Schulz cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| before #5468 | 179.8M elements | 12 | 71.0e12 |
| main | 572.1M elements (1.14 GB in bf16, per pipeline slot), all on rank 47 | 1 | 55.1e12 |
| this PR | 99.5M elements | 16 | 63.3e12 |

## What changed
- `_assign_balanced_compute_regions` caps the elements each rank computes in a bucket at the bucket's even share of redistributed elements plus its largest chunk. A rank that a chunk would take over the cap is skipped for that chunk, and the existing cost order picks among the other ranks.
- Why this cap: every chunk lands whole on one rank, so any placement leaves some rank with at least the even share or the largest chunk, whichever is larger, and the cap is within twice that. It is also the smallest cap that placement can always meet: until every chunk is placed, some rank holds less than the even share and can take any chunk.
- `test_shared_bucket_matches_per_parameter_buckets` used fixed work to pack every parameter onto rank 3, and the cap now spreads those chunks. `blocks` becomes (6, 6), so its 4-row and 2-row chunks still pack onto one rank, and the packing check accepts any rank. The final check gathers mismatches from every rank (see Testing).

## Alternatives considered
- Weigh all-to-all bytes against Newton-Schulz FLOPs: needs a hardware-specific exchange rate.
- A configurable cap: a user has no way to choose a value.

## Numerics
Placement only: each chunk runs the same Newton-Schulz on whichever rank computes it, so results are unchanged.

## Testing
- New `test_skewed_fixed_work_caps_compute_elements_per_rank` (CPU, fake process group): ranks 0-2 hold fixed work and rank 3 none, and four (16, 64) `Owned` parameters must compute 1024, 1024, 0 and 2048 elements per rank. Without the cap, all 4096 go to rank 3.
- Kimi K3 non-routed layers, the same 10 configs as the previous PR: plans are identical with and without the cap.
- `test_shared_bucket_matches_per_parameter_buckets` still catches both earlier bugs. With the return-region fix from #5695 removed, `layers.0.c` and `layers.0.blocks` differ on ranks 0-2. With DistMuon placing views as if the chunks were packed in reverse order, `layers.0.blocks` differs on ranks 0-2. Rank 3 stores no rows of `blocks` and computes neither `c` nor `blocks`, so it passed and then waited in teardown. The final check now gathers mismatches from every rank, so the test fails everywhere instead of hanging.
- `pytest tests/unit_tests/gpu/distributed/flex_shard/test_dist_muon.py tests/unit_tests/cpu/distributed/flex_shard`: 22 passed.

## Known issues / follow-ups
- In skewed buckets the cap trades Newton-Schulz balance for memory: in the example, the largest cost is 15% above main's. Step time is not measured.
- Chunks can still leave the ranks that store them (the review of #5468 found +10.7% all-to-all volume for Moonlight-16B at 32-way FSDP). This PR doesn't address that.
