## Agent details

**TLDR:** GDN's six input projections become one `in_proj` parameter, in the trainer and the generator. Its rows are grouped by key head, so a TP shard holds whole heads, as `QKVLinear` does for GQA. Qwen3.5-27B generator at TP4: decode -14% at 64 requests, mixed -2 to -3%. Trainer: same loss and grads within rounding for every GDN head layout in the repo.

```text
base:     x -> in_proj_q, _k, _v, _z, _a, _b    6 GEMMs -> conv -> recurrence   (the generator first cats q, k, v)
this PR:  x -> in_proj                          1 GEMM  -> split region -> conv -> recurrence

in_proj rows, per key head g (27B: 16 key heads x 1030 rows = 16480):
  [ q_g 128 | k_g 128 | v_g 384 | z_g 384 | a_g 3 | b_g 3 ]
TP4 Shard(0): rank r holds key heads 4r..4r+3, with all six projections
```

```python
# base: GDN.forward
query_TC = self.in_proj_q(x_TD)  # ... and five more GEMMs
# this PR
mixed_qkv_TC, a_TH, b_TH, gate_THV = _split_in_proj(
    self.in_proj(x_TD), self.in_proj_split_sizes, self.value_head_dim

```

**Problem**
1. **Six GEMMs.** GDN projects `x` six times (`in_proj_q`, `_k`, `_v`, `_z`, `_a`, `_b`). The generator then concatenates q, k and v for its paged convolution.
2. **Narrow ones.** `a` and `b` have 48 rows each at 27B, and cuBLAS splits the narrow GEMMs over K: 4 extra reduce kernels per layer, every step.
3. **Cost.** Qwen3.5-27B generator at TP4, decode with 64 requests: 9.0 ms per step, 48 GDN layers.
4. **Why not concatenate.** A plain `[q | k | v | z | a | b]` weight can't be TP-sharded: a `Shard(0)` would split blocks of different sizes across ranks.

**Solution**
1. **One parameter.** `GDN.in_proj` replaces the six, with rows grouped by key head (layout above), so `Shard(0)` keeps whole heads.
2. **One split.** A compiled region, `gdn_split_in_proj`, splits the output and copies q, k and v into one contiguous tensor for the convolution. The inner ops (`InnerGDN`, both CP variants, `VLLMInnerGDN`) take that tensor.
3. **Checkpoints.** The state dict adapter packs HF's four tensors (`in_proj_qkv`, `_z`, `_a`, `_b`) into `in_proj` and back, next to its QKV packing.
4. **Unchanged:** HF checkpoints, weight sync (the generator receives `in_proj` like any linear), the convolution weights, the other attention layers.

You may ask:
- Why not fuse only in the generator? Decode is a bit faster that way (-18% vs -14% at 64 requests: no q, k, v copy), but it needs a second copy of the weights, refreshed after every weight load, and an allocator override.
- Why group by key head, not concatenate per rank as vLLM does? A per-rank layout makes the global row order depend on the TP degree, so checkpoints need a TP-aware split. Grouping by key head gives one layout that any TP degree shards with `Shard(0)`; HF's Qwen3-Next stores its `in_proj_qkvz` the same way.
- Why copy q, k and v instead of passing views? vLLM's paged convolution reads one contiguous `[q | k | v]` block. The copy is the split region's 4 Triton kernels: 4.7-6.6 us per layer at 1-256 tokens (27B TP4).
- Why is the 35B-A3B trainer layer 1.3-1.6% slower? Likely the split's copies: at D 2048 the six GEMMs are already cheap (not profiled per kernel). 27B (D 5120) gains 1.2-2.8%.
- Why still three conv weights? One `[q | k | v]` conv weight would need the same key-head grouping and adapter packing as `in_proj`. The generator concatenates the three per call: 1.3 us per layer, about 0.8% of a 27B TP4 decode-64 step (48 layers, 7.76 ms). The trainer convolves three column views and pays nothing. Not worth the extra layout.

NOTE: TorchTitan DCP checkpoints saved before this PR have six keys per GDN layer and don't load into `in_proj`. HF checkpoints load as before, so the way out is to save with `checkpointer.last_save_in_hf=True` on the old commit and load with `checkpointer.initial_load_in_hf=True`.
NOTE: a model built from scratch draws one random `in_proj` instead of six, so its seed-42 losses differ from the base. Models loaded from HF are not affected.
NOTE: Ulysses CP splits `in_proj`'s output and re-concatenates q, k and v: one more copy on that path, not measured.
NOTE: MXFP8 `in_proj` at TP1 passes the `% 32` row check, but its blocks span section boundaries (1030 rows per key head). No recipe quantizes GDN projections.

**Verdict on @tianyu-l's review: correct, and it should block this design.** If the fused projection became a native parameter of `GDN`, the trainer and the generator would share it, and most of what this PR adds would go away. It isn't a drop-in change, though: the reason training is unfused today is TP head sharding, so the fused weight needs a head-group layout, as `QKVLinear` has.

(I couldn't post this as a PR review because this run has no permission for the review tool, so it's here instead.)

<details><summary>Review details</summary>

### Why the comment holds
- **There's a precedent.** `QKVLinear` (`torchtitan/models/common/attention/attention.py:121-191`) stores one native `wqkv` parameter. The HF adapter splits and merges it at the checkpoint boundary (`models/base/state_dict_adapter.py:257-345`).
- **A native fused weight would delete the generator-only machinery:**
  - `VLLMGDN` and its fused copies (`rl/generator/vllm/gdn.py:474-555`)
  - `free_storage` on FSDP-owned parameters
  - the refresh hook in `_unshard_and_free_sharded_params` (`model_wrapper.py:345-357`)
  - the private `torch._C._accelerator_setAllocatorSettings` override (`model_wrapper.py:484-504`)
  - the invariant that nothing may read the six projections between loads

  Weight sync would carry the fused key directly.
- **It matches the repo rules.** `.claude/rules/models.md` ("Training and Inference Share Modules") asks to avoid inference-only module variants, and `VLLMGDN` is one. Stack PR 6/6 (#5691) builds further on the fused copies.

### Why it isn't free
HF already ships `in_proj_qkv` and `conv1d` fused as `[all q | all k | all v]`. `qwen3_5/state_dict_adapter.py:309-337` splits them on purpose, because `Shard(0)` of that concatenation doesn't give each rank whole heads. The trainer runs TP1 and the generator TP4, so the native layout can't depend on the TP degree. It has to interleave per key head, as `QKVLinear` does per KV group:

```text
per key head g (27B: Hk=16, r=Hv/Hk=3, Dk=Dv=128):
  [q_g 128 | k_g 128 | v_g 384 | z_g 384 | a_g 3 | b_g 3]  = 1030 rows, x16 = 16480
Shard(0) at TP t -> 16/t whole groups per rank (qwen3_5/model.py:403 already requires t | Hk)
after the GEMM: view [T, Hk_local, 1030] -> split the last dim -> strided q/k/v/z/a/b
```

So each rank's `q|k|v` is no longer one contiguous block. Two options:
- **The generator pays one contiguous copy before the conv.** The base already pays that with its `cat`, so this keeps the 6->1 GEMM win but loses the "conv reads q|k|v in place" part.
- **The conv runs depthwise on the group-ordered channels**, with the conv weight and conv-state channel order fused the same way. Then the recurrence kernels need to accept strided head views. *Unverified.*

1. **@tianyu-l's comment is unanswered at HEAD.** Either measure a native head-group-fused `GDN` projection (and conv) on the 27B training step and on generator decode, or state in the `VLLMGDN` docstring and the PR body why training can't fuse. Today the docstring at `gdn.py:484` only explains why the generator copies (FSDP owns storage), not why the parameter isn't fused natively. If the native version lands, this PR becomes a much smaller change, and #5691 moves with it.

### What a native fusion costs in training
- **Wins:** 6->1 GEMM in forward, and 6->1 for both dgrad and wgrad in backward. The 48-column `a`/`b` GEMMs are the least efficient today. The three separate convs in `InnerGDN.run_stages` (`common/attention/gdn.py:373-390`) could also become 1 if the conv is fused too. Not measured.
- **Costs:**
  - contiguous copies after the split (`QKVLinear` already accepts these)
  - the HF adapter permuting to and from `in_proj_qkv`, `in_proj_z`, `in_proj_a`, `in_proj_b` and `conv1d`, plus an HF numerics re-check
  - new remat FQNs
  - a TP sharding config for the fused weight
  - regenerated qwen3_5 golden losses, since the fused GEMM is not bitwise equal to six GEMMs (per this PR's own numerics note)
