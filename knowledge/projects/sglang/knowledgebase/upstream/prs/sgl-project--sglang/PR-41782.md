# [PD] Register large host KV pools for RDMA in chunks, backed by 2 MiB pages

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/pull/41782) · state: closed · merged: False

## Original PR description

## Motivation

A host-memory KV pool on a PD decode can be hundreds of GiB. NICs with registration-size or address-translation limits reject a pool that large, through two limits:
- Under PD, Mooncake registers each contiguous run of a destination KV pool as one RDMA memory region, and a region that large fails to register.
- An RDMA NIC translates a registered region page by page. At 4 KiB pages a large pool can exhaust the NIC's translation budget, after which the NIC rejects further regions (ionic RDMA NICs fail with `EINVAL`). 2 MiB pages need 512 times fewer entries.

## Modifications

Both knobs are opt-in; with neither set, existing deployments are unaffected.
- `SGLANG_DISAGG_REGISTER_CHUNK_PAGES`: when set, each run is broken at multiples of that many pages of each buffer's item length; 0 or unset keeps whole runs, and a negative value is refused. The splitting lives in `chunk_register_regions` (`disaggregation/common/utils.py`), which refuses a non-positive step; `mooncake/conn.py`'s `_registerable_regions` uses it on the decode. The decode advertises its value at registration (a trailing field old peers ignore), and the prefill fails a transfer, naming both values, when its own value does not divide the decode's. Chunking refuses a non-KV buffer that overlaps a KV buffer (the unified KV/state pool), whose state transfers are not split at chunk boundaries.
- `SGLANG_HUGEPAGE_SIZE=THP` for the mmap host pool:
  - allocates private anonymous memory, rounded up to 2 MiB;
  - advises it with `MADV_HUGEPAGE`;
  - pre-faults it with `MADV_POPULATE_WRITE`, and refuses to run on a kernel without that (older than 5.14).
  - then reads the pool's `AnonHugePages` from `/proc/self/smaps`. If part of it is on 4 KiB pages, it runs `MADV_COLLAPSE` (Linux 6.1+) over the mapping before anything registers the pool. It logs the coverage at INFO, and fails the allocation if more than `SGLANG_HUGEPAGE_THP_MAX_SMALL_MB` (default 256; negative means no limit) is still on 4 KiB pages.

  hugetlbfs pages (`2MB`/`1GB`) need a reservation made ahead of time on every node; THP needs only THP in `madvise` or `always` mode. The mapping is private because shmem THP is often disabled while anonymous THP usually is not.

  On a node with fragmented memory, with THP defrag set to `madvise`, the pre-fault compacts memory and falls back to 4 KiB pages where compaction fails. A few GiB of 4 KiB pages are enough to exhaust the NIC. Measured with ionic RDMA NICs: with 4 GiB of a 259 GiB pool on 4 KiB pages, ionic rejected 1558 of the pool's 1845 regions of 144 MiB with `EINVAL`, starting at region 27 and including regions that were entirely on huge pages. With 25 GiB on 4 KiB pages, `MADV_COLLAPSE` restored huge pages in 4 s and all 1845 regions registered. Without the check, such a start fails later, as thousands of Mooncake registration errors.

A PD deployment with a large host KV pool uses both knobs together (register-chunking plus THP) to stay under both RDMA registration limits.

## Tests

CPU tests, on this head:
- `test_disaggregation_wire.py -k 'GroupConcurrentContiguous or ChunkRegisterRegions'`: 13 passed. New cases check that runs break at destination chunk boundaries and stay whole when the variable is unset; `TestChunkRegisterRegions` covers the helper, the decode's chunked registration, the refusal of an aliased KV/state buffer and of a negative value, and the prefill/decode compatibility rule; the registration test covers the new field.
- `test_mmap_allocator.py`: 14 passed (the real THP allocation is skipped on kernels without `MADV_POPULATE_WRITE`). New tests check that:
  - the THP pool is fully resident (`mincore`) and carries the `hg` VmFlag;
  - it refuses to allocate without `MADV_POPULATE_WRITE`;
  - the smaps accounting is correct;
  - a shortfall is collapsed;
  - a shortfall still left after the collapse fails the allocation, or is kept when it is within the limit or there is no limit;
  - a failing `MADV_COLLAPSE` is still followed by the check.

  Whether the kernel actually backs the region with huge pages depends on fragmentation, so the tests mock that part.
- `test/registered/unit/disaggregation` (whole directory): 529 passed, 17 skipped.
- A 64 GB THP allocation logs `59.61 GiB, 0.0 MiB on 4 KiB pages after the pre-fault` (3.3 s).
