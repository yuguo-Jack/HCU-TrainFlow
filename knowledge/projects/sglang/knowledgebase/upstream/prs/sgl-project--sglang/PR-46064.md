# [Perf] DSpark: Replay the static verify epilogue only on steps that take its accept

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/pull/46064) · state: closed · merged: False

## Original PR description

[by Claude Code]

> **Draft:** this has only run on unit tests and a 1-GPU synthetic benchmark. Before it is ready for review it needs a run on DeepSeek-V4.1 with DSpark (the `/rerun-test` command under Speed Tests) and a decision on the known regression after a post-startup recapture, described below.

## Motivation

DSpark is a speculative decoding method: a draft model proposes a block of tokens and the target model checks the
whole block in one "verify" forward. With a DeepSeek-V4.1 target in the default static verify mode (MoE draft, no
data-parallel attention), the target's verify CUDA graph ends with an epilogue that does two things:

- the greedy accept: an argmax over the vocabulary, then 3 broadcasts across the tensor-parallel (TP) ranks;
- the commit of the target hidden states into the draft's KV cache (GEMMs, then stores gated by a flag).

Only all-greedy steps use that result. On a step with sampled requests the graph still runs the whole epilogue
(the commit gated off), throws the result away, and then the step runs its own sampling accept, either eagerly or
in the opt-in sampling-accept graph from #45450.

In a torch profile of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at TP4 serving sampled requests, this dead span (from the epilogue's
argmax to the end of the verify graph) ran on every decode step. That profile was taken on an earlier build, before
this change.

## Modifications

- `dspark_verify.py`: on CUDA, the verify graph's capture hook now only records the graph's buffers (it still fills
  `static_io`, which the sampling-accept graph reads). After the target's graphs are captured at startup,
  `capture_static_graphs()` captures the unchanged `_static_epilogue` as its own small CUDA graph per verify graph,
  and `replay_static_epilogue()` replays it. The key is the batch size plus the addresses of the verify graph's
  logits and hidden-state outputs, because DeepSeek-V4.1 captures two verify graphs per batch size, which can have
  separate hidden-state buffers.
- `dspark_worker_v2.py`: `init_cuda_graphs` captures these graphs first (the scheduler calls it right after the
  target's capture). The verify step replays the epilogue graph right after the verify graph only on steps that
  take its accept (all-greedy, no grammar or logit processors, verify run as a graph). A step with no matching
  epilogue graph uses the eager accept. The first such step logs a warning, and the first replay logs an info line.
- Unchanged: ragged/compact verify, plus two cases that keep the epilogue inside the verify graph: static verify
  with `SGLANG_DSPARK_DEFER_CONTEXT_KV` (its KV projection forks inside the verify forward and joins in the
  epilogue), and ROCm, where the split is untested. No new flags.
- **Known regression against main:** the epilogue graphs are captured once. If the target's graphs are
  captured again after startup (a weight update with `recapture_cuda_graph=True`), all-greedy static steps use the
  eager accept until restart. Outputs are the same, but those steps are slower. On main the epilogue was recorded
  into the new verify graphs. Sampled steps and the sampling-accept graph behave as on main. Capturing the epilogue
  graphs again on a later step would fix this, but it adds a second capture path, so I left it out of this PR.

**Why outputs do not change.** On an all-greedy step the epilogue graph runs the same code on the same buffers right
after the verify graph, so its outputs are bitwise the same. A sampled step drops only work whose result nobody
read. The cost is one extra graph launch on all-greedy steps. Whether a step replays the epilogue graph depends on
step state that every TP rank shares, and on the startup capture, which every rank runs the same way (the verify
graphs' own TP collectives rely on the same thing). So the broadcasts inside the epilogue graph stay in step across
ranks.

**Related:** #45450 added the sampling-accept graph, which also runs as its own graph after the verify graph.
#42802 takes the other route and captures the sampled accept inside the verify graph.

Tests: new `test/registered/unit/spec/test_dspark_static_epilogue_graph.py` captures a stand-in verify runner both
ways (epilogue inside the verify graph, as before, and split) and checks:

- an all-greedy step gives bitwise-equal accept outputs and committed KV, at exact and padded batch sizes, for two
  graph variants per size, with and without `SGLANG_SIMULATE_ACC_LEN`;
- a sampled step's verify graph no longer writes either, and the 3 broadcasts moved into the epilogue graph;
- `static_io` has the same entries as before, at startup and after two recaptures;
- unknown output buffers fall back to the eager accept; after the two recaptures every step falls back, no records
  pile up, and the old verify outputs are released;
- `SGLANG_DSPARK_DEFER_CONTEXT_KV` and ROCm keep the epilogue in the verify graph;
- the worker replays the epilogue only on all-greedy steps whose verify ran as a graph.

- akhilg-nv · comment · 2026-10-23T23:41:57Z · [source](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/pull/46064#issuecomment-6376952103)

[by Claude Code] Closing this: what it saves is small next to what it would take to make it safe.

- **What it saves.** With a DeepSeek-V4.1 target in static verify mode (no DP attention), the verify graph ends with the greedy-accept epilogue: an argmax over the vocabulary, three broadcasts across the TP ranks, and the gated commit of the target hidden states into the draft KV cache. On a step with sampled requests that result is thrown away. In a torch profile at TP4 with sampled requests, this span was about 1.5-1.7% of a decode step. It exists only in static verify, only without DP attention and only on sampled steps, so ~1.5% is an upper bound at those points.
- **What it costs.** The PR moves the epilogue into its own CUDA graph, captured once at startup. After a later recapture (a weight update with `recapture_cuda_graph=True`), all-greedy steps fall back to the eager accept until restart. Fixing that needs a second capture path: re-capturing the epilogue graphs after a recapture, plus a check that every TP rank captured the same set, because the epilogue's broadcasts must stay in step across ranks. That adds roughly 40-80 lines to the CUDA-graph capture code, on top of this PR's +161 runtime lines.
- **Overlap.** #42802 takes the other route and captures the sampled accept inside the verify graph. Both change the same verify-graph code, so this would have to be reworked against it either way.

Worth revisiting if a profile at a deployed point shows the span is larger than this, or if #42802 does not land.
