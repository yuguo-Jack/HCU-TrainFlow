# [Perf] [NVIDIA] deep_select: Add a 3-CTA tier for the paged indexer top-k

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/pull/47328) · state: closed · merged: True

## Original PR description

## Motivation

`topk_select` runs one CTA per row, so it takes `ceil(rows / (SMs * occupancy))` rounds. At occupancy 2 on VR the grid quantizes to **2 × SMs rows**, and a DSv4.1 decode step at batch 128 asks for 512 — two full rounds for barely more than one round of work. The jump is visible in the raw timings: 512 rows costs 1.8× what 2 × SMs rows costs.

## What this does

Adds a third config to `TopkNormal` at occupancy 3, moving the grid to `3 * SMs`.

Fitting the plan under `(opt-in smem) / 3` caps `reconstruct_threshold` at 2048, which costs per-CTA throughput — so the tier is **taken only when it removes a round**. `TopkNormal` compares the two round counts and otherwise keeps the existing config.

A threshold that small leaves `NUM_EXTRA_SLOTS` under what the index-sort window delta table needs. That table is built only under `SORT_BY_INDEX`, so the assert is narrowed to the feature it protects rather than the bound being weakened — `MAX_VOCAB_SIZE` is untouched and no caller's vocabulary reach changes.

Three files, +18/-2.

## Measurements

VR (sm_100f), time per call after vs before, topk 512, `--check` against `torch.topk` at every point. The tier is read back out of the kernel's own template arguments, so this is the dispatch decision rather than an inference from timings:

| rows | rnd2 | rnd3 | tier chosen | after vs before |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 128 | - | - | `occ=1 rt=4096 d=5` | +0.9% |
| SMs | - | - | `occ=1 rt=4096 d=5` | +0.7% |
| 256 | 1 | 1 | `occ=2 rt=4096 d=4` | +0.2% |
| 2 × SMs | 1 | 1 | `occ=2 rt=4096 d=4` | +0.0% |
| 512 | 2 | 1 | **`occ=3 rt=2048 d=2`** | **-25.6%** |
| 3 × SMs | 2 | 1 | **`occ=3 rt=2048 d=2`** | **-29.6%** |
| 4 × SMs | 2 | 2 | `occ=2 rt=4096 d=4` | +0.3% |
| 1024 | 3 | 2 | **`occ=3 rt=2048 d=2`** | **-20.9%** |

<sub>vocab 65536. At vocab 131072 the same tiers are chosen and the wins are -27.7%, -25.8% and -14.9%.</sub>

Rows where `rnd3 == rnd2` keep the existing tier and land within 1% of the old time. 16/16 correct.

On a DSv4.1 decode step: **0.31% at batch 128, 0.19% at batch 256**. The benefit tracks rounds rather than batch size, so it is non-monotone — batches whose rows fall between 2 × SMs and 3 × SMs, or above 4 × SMs, gain; those between 3 × SMs and 4 × SMs do not.

## Scope

Measured on sm_107 only. Nothing in the mechanism is architecture-specific — every arch in `SUPPORTED_CUDA_ARCHS` reports the same 232448 B opt-in cap and the same register file, and the rule reads the SM count at runtime — but the per-CTA cost of the smaller threshold has not been measured on sm_90 or sm_100. The rule is self-limiting (it fires only when a round is saved, worth 33-50%, against a penalty measured at 8-14% here), so the sign should hold, but a confirmation on B200 or H100 would be welcome.
