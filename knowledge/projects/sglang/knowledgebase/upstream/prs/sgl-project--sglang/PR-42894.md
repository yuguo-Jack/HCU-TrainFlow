## Motivation

Kimi K3's decoder runs its own layer communication: completing o_proj's sum, the DP gather and scatter around the MLP and MoE, the pipeline handoff, the attention-residual bank's lifetime and the final aggregation. Most other models now leave all of that to stage boundaries (`append_stages`). This PR moves Kimi K3's standard residual path, its attention-residual bank path, and SP-MoE (an a2a MoE backend with attention TP, which runs each MoE on the rank's attention-TP shard) onto stage boundaries.

The remaining configurations keep the decoder's own communication for now:
- a dense MLP sharded over attention TP (`--enable-dense-mlp-attn-tp`);
- batches not padded to a multiple of attention TP (`--disable-attn-tp-gather`);
- the bank's fused all-reduce on multicast nodes;
- K3's tuned SP collectives;
- the mode that keeps the bank on each rank's shard (`SGLANG_K3_SP_ATTN_RES`).

The PRs above this one in the stack move them.

## Modifications

**Standard residual path**
- Each decoder layer appends an attention and an FFN stage. o_proj, the dense MLP and a non-latent MoE no longer sum their outputs; the attention's TP sum, the DP gather and scatter, the pipeline handoff and the final norm come from the boundaries and `residual_batch`.
- A dense layer declares the TP width its MLP is built with (`dense_tp_size`, the size of `mlp.down_proj`'s TP group). The MLP shards over the full TP group whatever `--moe-dense-tp-size` says, so with `--moe-dense-tp-size 1` the boundary still gathers every DP rank's rows for it and completes its sum over that group, as the decoder did.
- K3's latent MoE reduces its latent before the latent norm and completes the shared experts' sum beside it, so its output is complete when compute returns. `declare_ffn` gains `output_complete` for such a stage: its exit owes no sum.
  - `test_stage_producers_leave_sums` exempts the three functions that run those reductions, and only when the file declares `output_complete`.
- Input-scattered attention stays off for K3, as for Nemotron-H and LongCat: its forward never entered that scope.
- K3's `--boundary-reduction` default resolves to `ar`: under DP attention the FFN all-reduces and then scatters, as the decoder did. Its FFN input is read on each rank's rows once the attention's sum is complete, then gathered.

**Attention-residual bank**
- New `layer_boundary/residual/attn_bank.py`. Each read scores the banked snapshots and the running residual, mixes and normalizes them; a write layer also snapshots the residual its attention read forms, after which the running residual restarts empty.
- The update before each read is an ordinary add that the read folds into its aggregation. So the dense MLP and a MoE gathered over attention DP no longer add the pending prefix to their outputs.
- A latent MoE on the attention's own rows still adds the residual in its tail add and writes the next stream itself (`REPLACE_AT_EXIT`), so no separate add is launched.
- The bank lives for one forward:
  - The model opens it before the first layer, and every layer reaches it through a shared holder.
  - The stack's terminal read aggregates it before the final norm.
  - A later pipeline rank inherits the rows the earlier ranks banked. The sending rank adds up the stream's head and sends it beside the bank, now under its own key (`attn_res_bank`) instead of the residual's. The receiving rank takes that head as the written stream (`residual_batch.set_written`) and opens the bank with it, whatever the FFN before the handoff declares.
- Dynamic chunking's latency profile builds its stand-in PP proxy with the runner's buffer helper, so it carries the same keys and shapes as a real handoff.

**SP-MoE**
- The MoE layers take the boundaries' sharded FFN entry: the attention output's sum completes in a reduce-scatter over attention TP, the residual is sliced to the same rows, and the MoE runs on the shard.
- With the bank, the read aggregates the matching rows of the bank. The layer's exit gathers the stream back to every row (`exit_rows=ATTENTION`) because the bank is written on every row.
- **Behavior change on the standard residual path:** the layers follow the boundaries' usual sharded flow, as other models with an a2a MoE do: the residual stays on the shard and the next attention input is gathered. The decoder used to all-reduce the attention output and dispatch the whole batch from every attention-TP rank.
  - DSpark captures on this path move to where the stream is whole: the next attention input (`capture_gathered`) and the final norm. A final norm on a rank without rows still takes its capture.
- A reduce-scatter entry no longer slices an empty residual (a bank write layer's attention read leaves none).

**Tests**
- `test_attn_bank_residual_ops.py`:
  - the stack's reads and updates match the decoder's own order;
  - a pipeline rank continues from the head and the bank;
  - a read on a shard equals the matching rows of a full read;
  - the SP-MoE layers' plan (sharded entry, exit back to every row).
- `test_boundary_output_contracts.py` covers `output_complete`. `test_aux_capture_deferred_allreduce.py` covers the empty final-norm capture.

## Accuracy Tests

B200, compared with `main`. The model is a synthetic Kimi K3: the `moonshotai/Kimi-K3` config at full width (hidden 7168), 8 layers (MLA at 4 and 8, KDA elsewhere), 64 experts and dummy weights, in two variants, with and without the attention-residual bank. Runs use deterministic inference and NCCL, and dump each layer's output per rank.

The same tree already differs at layer 0 between some launches, so each check compares only pairs of runs whose layer-0 dumps match bit for bit. Every such pair must then match on every later layer and in the outputs.
- With the bank: TP 2 / TP 4 / PP 2, and SP-MoE with DeepEP at TP 2 × EP 2, TP 4 × EP 4, TP 4 × DP 2 × EP 4 and TP 2 × PP 2 × EP 2 are all identical to `main`.
- Standard residual path: TP 2 / TP 4 / PP 2 are identical to `main`.
- With the bank across a pipeline handoff, on B300 with the same model, deterministic inference: TP 4 × attention DP 2 × PP 2 without an a2a backend, and TP 2 × PP 2 with the handoff right after a dense layer. Both serve, and their outputs fall within `main`'s own launch-to-launch spread, with CUDA graphs and without (for the dense split, bit-identical to `main` without graphs).
- DP attention (TP 4 × DP 2), where launches of the same tree do not repeat bit for bit: each run's outputs fall into the clusters of `main`'s runs, with and without the bank, and with DeepEP for the bank.
- `--moe-dense-tp-size 1`, at the top of this stack on B300 (deterministic inference, same model): at TP 4, with and without the bank, the runs of both trees share one bit-identical group on every layer and in outputs (4 of 4 runs, and 3 of 4 without the bank). At TP 4 × DP 2 (attention TP 2), the largest bit-identical group over every layer after the first (the dense one, whose sum is still owed when the layer returns) and the outputs holds runs of both trees: 2 + 1 of 4 with the bank, 4 + 4 of 12 without. Declaring no width there instead leaves the dense layer's TP sum out: every prompt's output differs from `main`.
- Standard residual path with SP-MoE: different by design. Greedy tokens match, and logprobs differ by up to 7.8e-3, the same as between two launches of `main`.
- Kernel counts (profiler, per rank) are identical to `main` everywhere except two cases:
  - the standard residual path with SP-MoE, where the added all-gathers raise decode from 2117 to 2177 kernels;
  - under DP attention, the KDA chunked-prefill kernels' launch counts, which vary between launches on both trees.

## Speed Tests and Profiling

`sglang.benchmark.offline_throughput`, generated-shared-prefix (16 × 16 prompts, 512 + 512 input, 128 output), synthetic K3 with dummy weights, B200, measured at the top of this stack. Output tok/s, median of 2–6 launches:

| Config | `main` | This stack |
|---|---|---|
| TP 4, bank | 10742 | 10708 |
| TP 4, standard | 10808 | 10810 |
| TP 4 × EP 4 DeepEP, bank | 8174 | 8023 |
| TP 4 × EP 4 DeepEP, standard | 5720 | 8161 |

- The standard SP-MoE row is faster because each rank's MoE now runs on its shard instead of the whole batch.
- The −1.8% with the bank is host time: the kernels are identical, and the gap is in eager decode, where this benchmark's 256-request decode batches exceed the 128-request decode CUDA graphs that DeepEP's low-latency mode allows. Prefill and graph-captured decode are unchanged.
- KV capacity (`max_total_num_tokens`) is identical in every configuration. On B300, the whole run's peak allocated and reserved memory per rank is identical to the byte at TP 4 and SP-MoE TP 4 × EP 4, with the bank.
