## Motivation

Under `--enable-unified-memory`, the chain allocator ran the reclaim loop itself: it chose the victim order, built eviction requests keyed by the prefix tree's slot ids, read the tree's evictable and retained sizes and drove the tree's eviction walk, while the tree's `evict_for_alloc` called back into it. Capacity questions were asked in several shapes (three loose arguments, pages, tree-keyed maps), and only the target's window had a decode budget. This PR makes the allocator answer counts and geometry only, lets the prefix cache choose what to evict, and states every demand in one type keyed by sub-pool name.

## Modifications

- Reclaim owned by the cache: `mem_cache/unified_radix_cache.py::UnifiedRadixCache.evict_for_alloc` reclaims a demand on pools that share one memory with its own loop: take if the allocator can take it (rearranged if need be); evict nothing if even every evictable entry would not make it fit; otherwise evict in the cache's victim order and stop at the first victim after which the demand fits.
- Allocator surface: `mem_cache/kv/alloc/chain.py::ChainAllocator` exposes `units_fit`, `fits_after_frees` and `sub_pool_roles`; its tree reads, its reclaim loop (`_reclaim`, `reclaim_for_demand`, `_evict`) and its tree-slot rule are removed, along with its imports of the prefix cache and the prefill budget. `pools_share_memory` says which allocators reclaim jointly.
- One demand type: `mem_cache/kv/alloc/demand.py::KVDemand` (tokens, window tokens by sub-pool name, state slots) replaces the allocator's token demand and the cache's allocation demand; `evictable_by_sub_pool` reports what a cache could evict per sub-pool; the window reservation estimate moves beside it (`estimate_window_tokens`).
- Eviction counts by name: `EvictParams` / `EvictResult` carry `window_num_tokens` by sub-pool name; the tree maps names to its own slots when it walks.
- One admission entry: `ChainAllocator.admits` takes a `KVDemand`; `admits_units` is removed (answers unchanged: the evictable credit is floored to pages as before).
- Decode preallocation: allocators answer admission and readiness together (`admit_preallocation`): the chain admits on its one rule, crediting evictable entries, and reclaims what that credit counted on before saying yes.
- `mem_cache/capacity.py`: drives "evict, then check" through the cache and then asks the allocator (`prealloc_shortfall`, `units_fit`, `ensure_capacity`), and builds the prefill budget; decode preallocation, prefill load-back, decode capacity and the common eviction helper go through it.
- Window budgets: every window sub-pool of a decode preallocation is budgeted by one rule, by name (`window_budget_tokens`): its available plus evictable tokens, less the running and retracted requests' reserved growth (capped by the window's retained length).
- Tests: `test/registered/unit/mem_cache/test_unified_budget_trace.py` with a stored record of admission and budget answers for one to three window sub-pools and FULL+window / state+window+FULL chains (`SGLANG_RECORD_BUDGET_TRACE=1` rewrites it), `test_unified_window_budgets.py`, and the existing allocator, admission and eviction tests rewritten to the new interfaces.

**Behavior changes (unified memory only):**
- A unified chain of FULL and state (no window) now credits evictable cached entries when admitting a decode preallocation and reclaims them before the take, like every other chain.
- A unified pool with several window sub-pools (a fused draft window of another length) reserves running requests' growth in every window, so it admits a little less and retracts less. With one window, and for separate-buffer pools, every answer is unchanged. A window's growth is capped by its retained length (the window widened by a draft reader's reach), not the attention window.
- The reclaim victim order at this head is the cache's own in every mode (each short sub-pool's entries first, then token entries, then window entries). `main`'s preference for window entries first with a HiCache storage backend attached or on a PD decode node, and joint-LRU order for allocation reclaim, are restored in #48025.
