# [Fix] mem-cache: Budget the non-DSA EAGLE draft KV pool at its DCP-widened size

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/pull/39799) · state: closed · merged: True

## Original PR description

## Motivation

Under DCP a non-DSA EAGLE / MTP draft pool is allocated over the allocator's widened ids: `KVCacheConfigurator.loc_space_scale` is `dcp_size` for that draft worker, and `_derive_pool_sizes` allocates `max_total_num_tokens * dcp_size` draft rows per rank. `DefaultPoolConfigurator` prices the DSA draft term and the DFLASH draft term with `dcp_size`, but its non-DSA EAGLE / STANDALONE branch prices one row of the target's per-layer KV. For an MLA draft, whose latent is the size of the target's, that is `dcp_size` times too small: `max_total_num_tokens` comes out too large, and the static pools take `(dcp_size - 1)` draft layers' worth of KV per rank out of the memory that `--mem-fraction-static` leaves for activations and CUDA graphs. A GQA/MHA draft cannot be priced from the target's per-layer KV either way: `ModelConfig.get_num_kv_heads` replicates the target's KV heads over DCP but not the draft's.

This is the MTP + DCP deployment of MLA models such as DeepSeek-V3 / R1. On DeepSeek-R1 at TP8 / DCP8 the draft pool is 20.68 GB per GPU while the budget charges it 2.59 GB, so about 18 GB of the reserve is gone before the first request. The sizing code is shared by all platforms.

An MLA draft writes only its owner rows (`loc // dcp_size`), so most of its widened allocation is never written; #43599 sizes the MLA draft pool in physical rows. This PR does not change the allocation; it makes the budget price the draft rows the pool allocates today.

## Modifications

`DefaultPoolConfigurator`: price the non-DSA EAGLE / STANDALONE draft term as `dcp_size` rows per token, each at the draft's own per-layer KV size from `_compute_cell_size(..., dcp_size=1)`. For MLA that is the target's per-layer size; a GQA/MHA draft keeps its unreplicated KV heads. At `dcp_size == 1` nothing changes.

## Accuracy Tests

The patch changes only how many KV tokens the pools hold; no kernel or model path changes.

`test/registered/unit/model_executor/test_pool_configurator.py`: 71 passed, 36 subtests passed. `test/registered/unit/managers/test_dcp_logical_capacity.py`: 3 passed.

The new `test_eagle_dcp_replicated_draft_does_not_exceed_budget` runs an MLA model and a GQA model (8 KV heads, TP8) at `dcp_size` 1 and 4 and checks that the memory the pools take, with the draft at `dcp_size` rows of its own per-layer KV, stays within the budget and uses more than 99% of it. On `main` the MLA `dcp_size=4` subtest fails with `10907136 not less than or equal to 10000000`; pricing the GQA draft from the target's per-layer KV fails the GQA `dcp_size=4` subtest with `9157632 not greater than 9900000.0`.

## Pool Sizing Measurements

`amd/DeepSeek-R1-0528-MXFP4-v2` (61 layers + 1 MTP layer), MI355X, TP8, `--speculative-algorithm EAGLE --speculative-num-steps 3 --speculative-eagle-topk 1 --speculative-num-draft-tokens 4 --kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3`, default `--mem-fraction-static` (0.7718), with and without this patch. Numbers are TP0 startup logs with `--load-format dummy`. With the real weights at DCP 8 the token counts differ by fewer than 60 tokens (4,818,943 before, 4,330,064 after) and the free memory is the same.

| DCP | `max_total_num_tokens` before | after | Draft pool before | after | Free after pools before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5,035,505 | 5,035,505 | 2.70 GB | 2.70 GB | 63.43 GB | 63.43 GB |
| 4 | 4,842,479 | 4,618,980 (−4.6%) | 10.39 GB | 9.91 GB | 53.74 GB | 61.61 GB |
| 8 | 4,819,001 | 4,330,117 (−10.1%) | 20.68 GB | 18.58 GB | 43.69 GB | 61.76 GB |

"Free after pools" is the `Memory pool end. avail mem` line after the draft pool is allocated. Without the fix the unbudgeted draft rows take 7.9 GB (DCP 4) and 18.1 GB (DCP 8) out of that reserve.

The capacity change matches the layer count: the budget now prices `61 + dcp` layer-equivalents instead of `61 + 1`, and `4,842,479 × 62/65 = 4,618,980` and `4,819,001 × 62/69 = 4,330,117` match the measured values. The lower capacity is the cost of pricing the draft rows that are allocated today; the previous capacity was only reachable by spending the activation reserve.

## Speed Tests and Profiling

The patch only changes how many KV tokens the pools hold; no kernel or scheduling path changes.

No end-to-end throughput comparison: DeepSeek-R1 with EAGLE and DCP does not serve on ROCm `main`, with or without this patch. The non-DSA NextN draft keeps `dcp_size > 1` (`DeepseekV2AttentionMLA.__init__` only resets it for DSA drafts), so `forward_absorb_rocm_core` sends `draft_extend_v2` into the DCP decode branch with `k_window=None`, and `AiterAttnBackend.forward_extend` fails on `k.shape` while capturing the draft-extend CUDA graph. Both builds stop there, with dummy and with real weights. Pool sizing happens before that point, so the measurements above come from the full startup path.

## Reproduce

```bash
SGLANG_USE_AITER=1 python -m sglang.launch_server \
  --model-path amd/DeepSeek-R1-0528-MXFP4-v2 --trust-remote-code --tp 8 --dcp-size 8 \
  --speculative-algorithm EAGLE --speculative-num-steps 3 --speculative-eagle-topk 1 \
  --speculative-num-draft-tokens 4 --kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3 --load-format dummy
```

Compare the two `KV Cache is allocated` lines (target, then draft) and the `Memory pool end. avail mem` line that follows each, at `--dcp-size 1` and `--dcp-size 8`.
