# [Router] Expose cache-aware locality on /metrics: query / matched / selected blocks (2/3)

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/pull/39326) · state: closed · merged: False

## Original PR description

## Motivation

> **2 of 3** in the cache-locality stack, on top of **#39325**, which supplies
> the per-owner tier and block-0 presence these counters read. The KV
> storage-tier stack (**#39108** → **#39111**) is under it. Merge in order.

The router computes everything a router-side hit rate needs at the decision
point and only `debug!`-logs it (`chat.rs`, "cache candidate winner"). None of
it reaches `/metrics`: the 21 existing families expose which decision branch
fired, admission and pressure-guard counts, and per-worker load — but **no
prefix-depth signal at all**, so a hit-rate drop is undiagnosable from a scrape.

**The load-bearing part is the decomposition, not the counters.** A single
"cache hit rate" is the number that made this hard to reason about: the
router's view and the engine's disagree for four different reasons and one
ratio cannot tell them apart. Three counters sharing one `(model_id, decision)`
key can.

## Modifications

Seven families, all booked at **one** site once the winning worker is known — a
second tree lookup there would read a different tree, and three counters filled
from two places could disagree about which decision they belong to.

The triple, on `(model_id, decision)`:

| metric | what it answers |
|---|---|
| `sgl_router_cache_aware_query_blocks_total` | what the request asked about — the denominator |
| `sgl_router_matched_overlap_blocks_total` | what the **best** worker in the fleet holds — the ceiling a router with no load constraints could reach |
| `sgl_router_selected_overlap_blocks_total` | what the worker actually **taken** holds — the router's prediction of the engine's hit rate |

`matched - selected` is locality the routing decision gave up, attributable to
a decision bucket because all three book under one key. The comparison against
the engine's `cached_tokens_total / prompt_tokens_total` is **`selected /
query`, NOT `matched / query`** — the latter reads structurally high because it
meters the deepest prefix anyone holds even on selections that routed
elsewhere. That reading is in the module doc, which is the half of this change
that keeps the counters from being misread.

The residual is **not one-directional, and the direction is diagnostic.** It
runs high when the engine evicted what the tree still lists; it runs LOW when a
worker serves traffic while publishing no KV events — a `/server_info` probe
that failed at registration, or a page-size disagreement — which leaves that
worker fully routable and permanently absent from the tree. So `selected /
query` sitting *below* the engine's rate indicts the subscriber fleet, not
eviction.

Also here:

- **`sgl_router_cache_aware_decisions_total{model_id, decision}`** — one
  terminal outcome per cache-aware selection. The vocabulary is the set of
  outcomes the routing path can tell apart today: `cache_hit`, `no_candidates`,
  `candidates_exhausted`, `lookup_unavailable`. The last is the honest answer
  to "why is my hit rate 0" on a router that has not yet learned the fleet's
  page size. `no_candidates` and `candidates_exhausted` are kept apart
  deliberately — one is a cache problem, the other a sizing problem.
- **`sgl_router_overlap_blocks{model_id}`** — the distribution behind
  `matched`. Its `le="0"` bucket is the zero-match share.
- **`sgl_router_zero_match_block0_total{model_id, presence}`** — which splits
  that share by whether the request's FIRST queried block hash is carried
  anywhere in the tree. `absent` = the fleet never published it or removed it
  (engine-side); `in_tree` = carried, but the root-anchored walk could not
  reach it (router-side linkage). A zero match is the most common outcome on an
  unhealthy fleet and the two faults live in different repositories.
- **`sgl_router_selected_owner_tier_total{model_id, tier}`** — the cheapest
  tier the CHOSEN worker holds the prefix on, in the same vocabulary as
  `sgl_router_kv_tree_blocks`, so `host / (device + host)` says what fraction
  of affinity routes ride a load-back rather than an in-place hit.

The last two read the `tree_view` side-channel from **#39325**. It is `None` on
the out-of-process indexer path, so an indexer fleet reads `tier="unknown"`
rather than a fabricated `device`, and contributes nothing to the zero-match
split.

**The provider is not trusted for the nesting.** `selected` is clamped to
`matched` and `matched` to `query` at the booking site. The in-process tree
derives both from one descent and cannot disagree, but the out-of-process
indexer computes `best_prefix_blocks` and the per-worker depths separately, on
a wire contract that only documents the relationship. A hit rate above 100% is
a poor way to learn an indexer is out of contract. The `debug_assert` stays
rather than being made redundant by the clamp: the clamp keeps a release
build's charts honest, the assertion is what fails a test build loudly enough
to go fix the provider.

## On the names

`sgl_router_cache_aware_query_blocks_total` next to
`sgl_router_matched_overlap_blocks_total` is untidy, and metric names are
permanent. These are kept anyway: all seven families are **already emitted
under these names** by the deployed fork this stack is converging onto
upstream, and renaming them here would break its dashboards at the moment
upstream takes over.

`sgl_router_overlap_blocks` returns under a **narrower** meaning than the one
removed with the `cache_aware_zmq` policy — it is the fleet-best overlap, not
what the chosen worker holds. Queries that read its `_sum` as a hit rate were
already reading the ceiling; `monitoring/README.md` points them at
`selected / query` instead.

## Diagnosis path this unlocks

| observation | conclusion |
|---|---|
| `selected/query` fell, `matched/query` flat | the load gate is diverting off the prefix owner |
| both fell | the tree or the indexer is losing state |
| router ratios flat, engine hit rate fell | engine-side eviction, not routing |
| `selected/query` **below** the engine's rate | a worker serves traffic while publishing no KV events |
| `zero_match_block0_total{presence="absent"}` rising | engine-side publish gap, not a router linkage fault |

## Tests

The triple booked under one decision key, with the capacity bucket's `selected`
kept out of the hit bucket; a lookup-less selection contributing a decision and
no blocks; the histogram observing `matched` and keeping its zero bucket; a
`should_panic` on a selection that beats the fleet best, so a call site that
swaps two counts fails at the booking site rather than rendering above 100% in
Grafana; a provider overshooting the query clamped; a holder reported deeper
than the fleet best clamped; the selected worker's own tier reported rather
than the fleet best's; `unknown` without a tree view; and every new family
rendering `model_id` through `escape_label`, since one unescaped line takes the
whole scrape with it, not just that series.
