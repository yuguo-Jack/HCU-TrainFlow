- hjh0119 · comment · 2026-05-30T15:44:31Z · [source](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/pull/9035#issuecomment-4583312409)

I would advise against supporting cross-tokenizer distillation for a couple of reasons:

1. To my knowledge, current frontier model research and academic work primarily focus on distillation between models of the same family (i.e., same tokenizer).
2. Introducing cross-tokenizer support would add significant code complexity, regardless of whether it is integrated into the existing `GKDTrainer` or implemented as a separate Trainer.

The current `GKDTrainer` has already accumulated a considerable number of features. In the next iteration, I plan to refactor it to simplify the codebase and explore a design that supports multi-teacher distillation scenarios. As such, I would recommend holding off on further development in this direction for now.

That said, thank you for your contribution — it is very much appreciated!

Agreed, though there are algorithms such as GOLD that support cross-tokenizer distillation. However, it is essentially just log-probability alignment, that is, making the log-probability distributions as similar as possible. This kind of distribution alignment is not syntax-related, so it brings little gain after distillation. We tested cross-tokenizer distillation from Gemma 4 to Qwen on the IF dataset, and IFEval showed almost the same performance.
