Fixes #<!-- issue number -->

Add support for LoRA supervised fine-tuning of `allenai/Olmo-3-7B-Think` on Ascend NPU through the native Transformers training pipeline.

This PR includes:

- Model registration with `--model_type olmo3`.
- An `olmo3_think` template that follows the official Think conversation format, including system prompts, thinking content, and response boundaries.
- NPU adaptations for RMSNorm and SwiGLU MLP, with fallback paths where required, while preserving native FP32 RoPE computation.
- SDPA and eager attention support that preserves Olmo3's sliding-window and full-attention masks.
- Explicit checks for unsupported NPU FlashAttention, packing, padding-free training, and sequence parallelism.
- Regression tests covering model loading, conversation formatting, attention masks, forward/backward computation, and LoRA training.
- A single-node 16-device LoRA SFT example and a Chinese README covering environment setup, dataset preparation, training, and validation.

The example supports pure-text conversations. Tool calling, multimodal inputs, and the Megatron backend are outside the scope of this PR.

## Experiment results

Environment:

| Component | Version |
| --- | --- |
| PyTorch | 2.10.0+cpu |
| torch_npu | 2.10.0.post4 |
| Transformers | 4.57.1 |
| CANN | 9.1.0 |

The example script is configured with:

- BF16 and SDPA attention.
- LoRA rank 8 and alpha 32.
- Gradient checkpointing.
- Maximum sequence length of 4096.
- Per-device batch size of 1 and gradient accumulation of 2.
- 16 devices, giving an effective global batch size of 32.

We compared LoRA supervised fine-tuning of `allenai/Olmo-3-7B-Think` on NVIDIA A100 and Ascend A3, and evaluated the effect of the NPU RMSNorm and SwiGLU patches on training metrics and runtime.

### Compared implementations

| Run | Platform | RMSNorm and SwiGLU implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A100 | NVIDIA A100 GPU | GPU reference implementation |
| A3-pre | Ascend A3 NPU | Without the NPU RMSNorm and SwiGLU patches |
| A3-post | Ascend A3 NPU | With the NPU RMSNorm and SwiGLU patches |

`A3-pre` and `A3-post` denote the unpatched and patched NPU runs, respectively. The patch comparison concerns RMSNorm and SwiGLU; it does not denote a change to the model architecture.

### Comparison of logged training metrics

For each pair of runs, we aligned the logs by training step and computed the absolute difference at each shared logged step. Mean absolute error (MAE) is the mean of these differences, and maximum absolute error is their maximum. The A3-pre vs. A3-post values were computed directly from the two NPU logs, rather than by subtracting their respective errors against A100.

Loss and gradient-norm errors are reported on their original, unitless numerical scales; they are not percentages. `grad_norm` refers to the scalar gradient norm recorded in the training logs.

| Metric | A100 vs. A3-pre | A100 vs. A3-post | A3-pre vs. A3-post |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Loss MAE (unitless) | 0.000399 | 0.000411 | 0.000012 |
| Loss maximum absolute error (unitless) | 0.001171 | 0.001362 | 0.00038 |
| Gradient-norm MAE (unitless) | 0.0039167 | 0.004024 | 0.000107 |
| Gradient-norm maximum absolute error (unitless) | 0.01224 | 0.012341 | 0.01578 |

Relative to the A100 reference, the patched A3 run has a loss MAE of **0.000411** and a gradient-norm MAE of **0.004024**. The direct comparison between the unpatched and patched A3 runs yields a loss MAE of **0.000012** and a gradient-norm MAE of **0.000107**.

These results quantify differences in logged loss and gradient-norm values during training. They do not establish elementwise agreement of logits or gradient tensors, or downstream task accuracy; no numerical acceptance threshold is applied to these measurements.

### Training runtime

Each run completed 2,000 training steps. In addition to the full-run timing, we report the 50-step interval bounded by steps 1,245 and 1,295, selected after loss stabilization. The interval runtime is the elapsed-time difference between those two steps, and its mean step time is the interval runtime divided by 50.

| Metric | A100 | A3-pre | A3-post |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Total runtime for 2,000 steps (s) | 2459.89 | 2586.1068 | **2260.7432** |
| Mean step time over 2,000 steps (s/step) | 1.229945 | 1.293053 | **1.130372** |
| Runtime for the 50-step interval, steps 1,245 to 1,295 (s) | 61 | 67 | **57** |
| Mean step time over the 50-step interval (s/step) | 1.22 | 1.34 | **1.14** |

Enabling the NPU RMSNorm and SwiGLU patches reduced the A3 full-run runtime from **2586.1068 s** to **2260.7432 s**, a **12.58% reduction** and a **1.144x speedup**. In the reported 50-step interval, runtime decreased from **67 s** to **57 s**, a **14.93% reduction** and a **1.175x speedup**.

Runtime reduction is calculated as `(A3-pre time - A3-post time) / A3-pre time`; speedup is calculated as `A3-pre time / A3-post time`. These figures describe the measured training runs, rather than a hardware-normalized comparison between A100 and A3.

感谢建议。我理解复用现有模板检查、避免新增接口的考虑。

保留这个校验入口的原因是：当前 SFT 流程会先加载模型并调用 sequence_parallel.prepare()，之后才创建模板。因此，仅依赖现有模板检查，会在这些操作之后才发现不支持的配置。另外，用户可以通过 --template 替换模板，从而绕过 Olmo3 模板中的专用检查，但模型本身的混合注意力限制仍然存在。

当前入口能够独立于模板选择，在模型加载和 sequence parallel 准备之前拒绝这些配置。SftArguments 只负责将校验分发给对应 loader；没有覆盖该方法的 loader 默认不执行任何操作。回归测试覆盖了 olmo3 和 default 两种模板，并验证不支持的配置不会进入模型加载和 SP 准备流程。

基于这两个原因，我建议保留该入口，同时继续保留现有模板检查。
