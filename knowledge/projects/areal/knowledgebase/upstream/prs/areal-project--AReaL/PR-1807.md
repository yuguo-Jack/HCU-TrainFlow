# fix(utils): split fused HCCL P2P batches by directed peer

[Upstream PR](https://github.com/areal-project/AReaL/pull/1807) · state: open · merged: False

## Original PR description

## Description

Large AWEX weight updates can hang when the torch-npu compatibility shim forwards thousands of operations to one fused HCCL call, including repeated sends or receives for the same peer. Split the operations into matching rounds with at most one operation per direction and peer, preserving per-peer ordering and batching independent peers together. Keep the existing grouped-P2P version gate and return every round's work handle.

## Related Issue

No linked issue. Reproduced with torch-npu 2.10.0.post4 and awex-ascend 0.2.0.

## Additional Context

- `python -m pytest tests/test_torch_npu_compat.py -q`: 5 passed, covering uneven peer counts, matching round indices, per-peer ordering, and unchanged single-round batches.
- `pre-commit run --all-files`: passed.
- NPU diagnostics: the original fused call hung at approximately 9,200 operations per rank; the patched path passed repeated transfers with exact received-value checks on 4 and 16 NPUs. The installed AWEX wrapper also passed.
- Full-model validation: two consecutive Qwen3.6-35B-A3B updates completed with batching enabled on eight training and eight inference ranks. All writers/readers passed both completion barriers and training continued.
- Transport-only benchmark: five measured repetitions after warmup, alternating individual and patched batched methods on 16 NPUs, showed 9.7–10.1x lower transfer time for patched batching with contiguous BF16 tensors of 1 KiB, 64 KiB, and 1 MiB. This excludes conversion, noncontiguous copyback, inference weight loading, and concurrent training; it is not an end-to-end update speedup claim.
- Remaining validation: multi-node transport, colocated transfers, and custom-stream asynchronous callers were not qualified. The patch affects the global NPU fused fallback; additional HCCL launches and work handles may affect other repeated-peer workloads.
